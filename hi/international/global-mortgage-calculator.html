      Formula and calculation assumptions

      For a fully amortising fixed-rate loan, the payment for the selected repayment interval is:

      Payment per period = P × i × (1 + i)N ÷ ((1 + i)N − 1)
      • P is the property price less the down payment.
      • i is the periodic interest rate, converted from the entered nominal annual rate using the selected compounding convention.
      • N is the mortgage term in years multiplied by the selected payments per year.
      • At 0% interest, the principal is divided evenly over all scheduled payments.
      • The monthly-equivalent principal-and-interest amount is the payment per period multiplied by payments per year and divided by 12.
      • Optional annual property tax and insurance are divided by 12; monthly maintenance is added as entered.

      This is a general mathematical estimate, not a jurisdiction-specific mortgage quote. Mortgage products, payment frequencies, rate resets, tax rules, transaction costs, insurance requirements, eligibility tests and consumer protections vary by country and sometimes by state, province or region. Those rules are not automatically looked up or applied here. Select the compounding and payment frequency shown in your lender's documents.

      Canada convention: The Canada preset uses a nominal annual rate compounded semi-annually, with monthly payments by default. The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada shows this convention in its federally regulated mortgage information-box example. Your lender's contract takes precedence. See the official Canadian mortgage disclosure example. FCAC also explains monthly, semi-monthly, biweekly and weekly payment options in its mortgage guide.

      Frequently asked questions

      Can I use this calculator outside India, the US and the UK?

      Yes. Select a country or territory, confirm the currency code, and enter the price, deposit, annual rate and term in that currency. The formula is a general monthly amortisation estimate.

      Does the country selector find mortgage rates?

      No. It suggests a currency for the selected market. Enter a rate from a lender illustration or use a hypothetical rate for scenario analysis.

      Does it calculate local stamp duty, transfer tax or closing costs?

      No. These charges differ by jurisdiction and transaction. Check the relevant local authority and lender, and include a cost only if you know the amount and want it in the monthly estimate.

      Can I change payment frequency and interest compounding?

      Yes. You can model monthly, semi-monthly, biweekly or weekly payments and daily, monthly, quarterly, semi-annual or annual interest compounding. Match your lender's agreement; conventions vary by market and lender.

      Does it support interest-only or variable-rate mortgages?

      No. This version models a fully amortising loan with a fixed assumed annual rate and equal scheduled payments. Interest-only, variable-rate, offset, balloon and lender-specific products are not included.
