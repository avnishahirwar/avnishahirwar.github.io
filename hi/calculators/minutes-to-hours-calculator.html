How to use this calculator

Enter a non-negative number of minutes. Fractional minutes are supported. The calculator converts the same duration into decimal hours and complete hours plus remaining minutes.

Example: 150 minutes ÷ 60 = 2.5 hours, which is 2 hours and 30 minutes.

Formula and method

Decimal hours = minutes ÷ 60. Whole hours = floor(minutes ÷ 60); remaining minutes = minutes mod 60.

Understanding the result

Decimal hours are useful when a spreadsheet, timesheet or estimate expects a single number. The hours-and-minutes result is easier to read as a clock-style duration. For example, 95 minutes is 1.5833 decimal hours (rounded to four places in the display) and 1 hour 35 minutes. The calculator formats the displayed numbers for readability.

Using fractional minutes

The input accepts decimal values, so 12.5 minutes is valid. Divide 12.5 by 60 to get approximately 0.2083 hours. The breakdown remains 0 hours and 12.5 minutes; it does not convert seconds into a separate field. The display rounds the remaining minutes to two decimal places and decimal hours to four places.

When to use each result

Use decimal hours to add durations in a spreadsheet or compare time estimates in a consistent unit. Use hours plus minutes when communicating a duration to another person. If you add multiple entries, convert them to the same unit first and round only at the stage required by your own timesheet or billing rules.

Important limitations

This tool converts units only. It does not determine payroll, overtime, billable hours or contractual working time. Apply the relevant workplace or service rules separately.

Frequently asked questions

How many minutes are in one hour?

One hour contains 60 minutes.

How do I convert 150 minutes?

150 ÷ 60 = 2.5 hours, or 2 hours and 30 minutes.

Can I enter fractional minutes?

Yes. Enter a decimal value such as 12.5 minutes.

What is 95 minutes in hours?

95 divided by 60 is approximately 1.5833 hours, or 1 hour and 35 minutes.

Does the calculator round my payroll time?

No. It converts a duration only. Follow your employer’s timekeeping and rounding rules separately.
