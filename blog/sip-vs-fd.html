SIP vs FD: Understand the Difference Before Comparing

A practical guide to two very different financial products

SIP and FD are often compared because both can involve regular saving. But they are not the same kind of product. A systematic investment plan (SIP) is a method of investing periodically in a mutual fund. A fixed deposit (FD) is a deposit product offered by a bank or other eligible institution with defined interest and maturity terms.

Key point: An SIP does not guarantee a return. Mutual fund values can go up or down. An FD has stated terms, but deposit protection and early-withdrawal rules have limits.

1. How each works

SIP: You contribute a chosen amount at regular intervals to a selected mutual-fund scheme. The units purchased depend on the scheme’s NAV on the relevant transaction date. Outcomes depend on the underlying assets and market conditions.

FD: You place a lump sum for a selected tenure at an offered interest rate. Interest may be paid periodically or compounded, depending on the product. Premature withdrawal may affect the interest payable and may involve a penalty under the bank’s terms.

3. Why SIP returns are not guaranteed

Mutual funds hold securities whose prices and interest rates can change. The NAV may rise or fall, and investors may receive less than the amount contributed. AMFI states that mutual-fund schemes are not guaranteed or assured-return products and that past performance does not guarantee future performance. Read the scheme information document and riskometer before making any decision.

Official reference: AMFI — Risks in Mutual Funds.

4. What deposit insurance covers

DICGC insures eligible bank deposits up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank, including principal and interest, in the same right and capacity. This is an aggregate limit across eligible accounts at that bank—not ₹5 lakh for every FD or branch. Check the institution’s eligibility and DICGC’s official explanation.

Official reference: Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation.

6. Questions to ask before comparing

  • Do you need a known maturity amount or can you tolerate market fluctuations?
  • What are the withdrawal, lock-in, exit-load or premature-closure terms?
  • How will tax affect the amount you actually receive?
  • Is the return shown by a calculator guaranteed, or merely an assumption?

There is no universal winner. These products serve different purposes and carry different risks. This article is for financial education, not an instruction to invest or a personal recommendation.

Frequently asked questions

Is an SIP a type of investment?

An SIP is a way to invest a fixed amount at regular intervals in a mutual fund scheme; the scheme itself determines the underlying investments and risks.

Are SIP returns guaranteed?

No. Mutual fund returns are market-linked and are not guaranteed or assured. The value can rise or fall, and principal can be at risk.

Is a bank FD risk-free?

A bank FD has a stated interest rate and maturity terms, but it still carries bank and product terms. DICGC deposit insurance applies only within its rules and limits.

Can I compare an SIP with an FD calculator?

You can compare illustrative cash flows, but assumptions about mutual-fund returns are not guaranteed and should not be treated as forecasts.
