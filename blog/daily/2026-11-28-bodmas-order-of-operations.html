BODMAS vs PEMDAS: Order of Operations Examples

28 November 2026 · Friendly Calculator Editorial Team · AI-assisted article; not independently reviewed by a human editor

BODMAS and PEMDAS are memory aids for the same basic idea: evaluate an arithmetic expression in a consistent order. The letters can make it look as if division must always happen before multiplication, or addition before subtraction. That is not the rule. Multiplication and division share one level of priority; addition and subtraction share another. Within each pair, work from left to right.

The order of operations

  1. Grouping symbols: evaluate expressions inside brackets or parentheses first, starting with the innermost group.
  2. Orders or exponents: evaluate powers and roots where they appear in the expression.
  3. Multiplication and division: work from left to right, completing whichever operation appears first.
  4. Addition and subtraction: work from left to right, completing whichever operation appears first.

BODMAS is often expanded as Brackets, Orders, Division, Multiplication, Addition, Subtraction. PEMDAS is Parentheses, Exponents, Multiplication, Division, Addition, Subtraction. The paired operations have equal priority in both systems; the left-to-right rule resolves their order.

Worked examples

Example 1: multiplication before addition

12 + 6 × 2

Multiply first: 6 × 2 = 12. Then add: 12 + 12 = 24.

Example 2: brackets change the grouping

(12 + 6) × 2

Evaluate the brackets first: 12 + 6 = 18. Then multiply: 18 × 2 = 36.

Example 3: division and multiplication go left to right

18 ÷ 3 × 2

Division appears first from the left: 18 ÷ 3 = 6. Then multiply: 6 × 2 = 12. Do not multiply 3 × 2 first.

Example 4: addition and subtraction go left to right

24 − 8 + 3

Subtract first from the left: 24 − 8 = 16. Then add: 16 + 3 = 19.

Example 5: combine powers and multiplication

2³ + 4 × 2

Evaluate the power: 2³ = 8. Multiply: 4 × 2 = 8. Add the results: 8 + 8 = 16.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Reading the mnemonic as six separate priority levels. Division is not automatically ahead of multiplication, and addition is not automatically ahead of subtraction.
  • Ignoring brackets. A pair of parentheses can change the result by changing which operation is grouped first.
  • Moving from right to left when operations have equal priority. For multiplication/division and addition/subtraction, use left to right.
  • Leaving an expression ambiguous. Add brackets or split it into steps when a negative sign, power or implied multiplication could be read in more than one way.

Division by zero is undefined. If an expression contains a zero divisor, stop rather than treating it as an ordinary calculation.

Frequently asked questions

Is BODMAS different from PEMDAS?

No. They are different mnemonics for the same hierarchy of grouping, powers, multiplication/division and addition/subtraction.

Does multiplication always come before division?

No. They have equal priority. Complete them from left to right as they appear.

Does addition always come before subtraction?

No. They also have equal priority. Complete them from left to right.
