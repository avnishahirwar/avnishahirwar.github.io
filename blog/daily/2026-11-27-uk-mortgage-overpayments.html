UK Mortgage Overpayments: Allowances, Charges and Interest Savings

27 November 2026 · Friendly Calculator Editorial Team · AI-assisted educational content

A mortgage overpayment reduces the outstanding balance. It may lower future interest, but the result depends on the mortgage contract and the lender's payment rules. A calculator models the arithmetic; it cannot confirm whether a lender will accept a payment without a charge.

Check the lender's rules before paying extra

There is no single overpayment allowance that applies to every UK mortgage. The amount, timing and charge rules can vary by lender, product and rate period. Read the mortgage offer and current product terms, then ask the lender to confirm anything that is unclear.

  1. Find the allowance. Check whether the product permits regular or lump-sum overpayments and how the permitted amount is calculated. The offer may refer to the original balance, current balance or another basis.
  2. Check the reset date. The allowance may reset by calendar year, mortgage year or another date in the agreement. Confirm the deadline and whether unused allowance carries forward.
  3. Read the charge wording. Look for an early repayment charge (ERC), when it can apply and how it is calculated. Do not assume a charge applies only to the amount above an allowance.
  4. Confirm payment allocation. Ask whether an extra payment reduces the term, lowers the next monthly payment, or gives you a choice. Those outcomes produce different schedules.
  5. Keep a record. Request an updated balance or repayment illustration after the lender processes the payment.

How a monthly overpayment changes the estimate

A standard repayment estimate uses the outstanding principal, monthly interest rate and number of monthly payments. With a constant nominal annual rate:

Monthly rate = annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100
Payment = principal × monthly rate ÷ (1 − (1 + monthly rate)−number of months)

Each month, estimated interest is the opening balance multiplied by the monthly rate. The scheduled payment covers that interest first; the rest reduces principal. This example applies the extra payment to principal while the scheduled payment stays unchanged, then adjusts the final payment to clear the balance.

For an illustration, assume £180,000 outstanding, a constant annual rate of 4.25%, 25 years remaining and £100 extra each month. The scheduled payment is about £975.13. Without extra payments, estimated interest is £112,538.57 over 300 months. With the additional £100 each month, estimated interest is about £93,335.68 over 255 months.

Under those assumptions, the model shows about £19,202.89 less interest and a payoff 45 months earlier. This is an arithmetic illustration, not a lender quote. Daily-interest methods, payment dates, rate changes and lender allocation rules can change the actual result.

Compare estimated savings with any charge

A first comparison is estimated interest reduction minus any applicable ERC and administration or transaction fees. Treat this as a screening calculation, not a complete financial decision: it does not include every product term, tax consideration, emergency-cash need or alternative use of money.

Ask the lender for the exact charge calculation and an updated illustration. If the lender offers a choice between reducing the term and reducing the monthly payment, request both schedules. A lower monthly payment and a shorter repayment term are different outcomes.

Frequently asked questions

Can every UK mortgage be overpaid?

No universal allowance applies. Check the mortgage offer and ask the lender what can be paid without an ERC.

Does this calculator include an early repayment charge?

No. It models principal and interest only. Confirm any charge separately with the lender.

Will an overpayment always shorten the term?

No. This calculator assumes the scheduled payment stays unchanged. A lender may instead recalculate the monthly payment or apply a different rule.
