How to Calculate Exam Marks with Negative Marking

26 November 2026 · Friendly Calculator · AI-assisted article

When a multiple-choice paper deducts marks for incorrect answers, the number of correct responses is not the final score. Subtract the penalty for wrong answers and keep accuracy separate from the number of questions attempted.

Open the Negative Marking Calculator to enter your paper's own scoring rules.

The negative-marking formula

Let C be correct answers, W incorrect answers, M marks awarded per correct answer and P the penalty per incorrect answer. Enter P as a positive amount.

Net score = (C × M) − (W × P)

For T total questions, unanswered questions equal T − C − W. This general model gives unanswered questions zero marks. Partial credit, bonus questions, section-specific rules and score floors need separate treatment.

Worked example: a 100-question practice paper

Suppose there are 100 questions, 62 correct answers and 18 incorrect answers. Each correct response is worth 4 marks, and each incorrect response deducts 1 mark.

MeasureCalculationResult
Marks earned62 × 4248
Penalty18 × 118
Net score248 − 18230
Attempted62 + 1880
Unattempted100 − 8020

The maximum possible score under this simple scheme is 100 × 4 = 400. The net score is 230 out of 400, or 57.5% of the maximum marks.

Accuracy and attempt rate are different

Accuracy uses attempted questions as its denominator. It answers: among the questions attempted, what share were correct?

Accuracy = Correct ÷ Attempted × 100

Here, accuracy is 62 ÷ 80 × 100 = 77.5%. Attempt rate uses the full paper: 80 ÷ 100 × 100 = 80%.

A high attempt rate does not automatically mean a high score. Read net score, accuracy and attempt rate together. Accuracy does not measure question difficulty or guarantee future results.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the total questions in the paper.
  2. Enter correct and incorrect answers. Their sum cannot exceed the total.
  3. Enter marks awarded for a correct answer.
  4. Enter the penalty as a positive number. If there is no negative marking, enter zero.
  5. Calculate net score, accuracy, attempt rate and unanswered count.

Check the official marking scheme first

Do not assume every entrance or recruitment examination uses the same marks or penalty. Read the current official information bulletin for the exact exam, year, paper and section. Confirm whether unanswered answers are neutral, whether multiple-select questions have partial credit, and whether special questions use different rules.

This tool is a general arithmetic aid. It does not calculate rank, percentile, normalized marks, cut-offs or admission chances. Those outcomes may depend on paper difficulty, candidate performance, session normalization and other official rules.

Frequently asked questions

Is a penalty of minus one entered as 1 or -1?

Enter 1. The calculator treats the penalty input as a positive deduction and subtracts it from marks earned.

Are unanswered questions included in accuracy?

No. Accuracy uses attempted questions only. Unanswered questions are included in the attempt-rate denominator, not the accuracy denominator.

Can the net score be below zero?

Yes. The formula can return a negative number if penalties exceed marks earned. Check whether the official exam applies a minimum score of zero or another special rule.

Does this predict a JEE or NEET percentile?

No. It calculates a raw score from the values entered. Percentile and rank depend on exam-specific official processes and candidate data.
