Business Loan EMI: Formula, Example and Total Cost Checks

27 November 2026 · Friendly Calculator Editorial Team · AI-assisted educational content

A business-loan EMI estimate can explain the monthly repayment on a standard reducing-balance term loan. It is only one part of the borrowing cost. The rate type, fees, repayment schedule and product structure also matter, so compare the lender's documents rather than relying on one monthly figure.

When a standard EMI formula is suitable

The usual EMI formula is designed for a loan with a known principal, a constant periodic rate and equal instalments paid at regular intervals. Each instalment covers the interest due for that period and reduces the outstanding principal. As the balance falls, the interest portion usually falls and the principal portion rises.

This model is not a complete calculation for every business-finance product. A cash-credit or overdraft facility, bullet repayment, seasonal instalment plan, moratorium, staggered disbursement or changing rate needs a schedule that matches those terms. Do not treat a simple EMI estimate as confirmation of eligibility or approval.

The reducing-balance EMI formula

For a monthly schedule, convert the annual rate into a monthly decimal rate and use the total number of monthly instalments:

Monthly rate (r) = annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100
Number of payments (n) = tenure in years × 12
EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

P is the amount borrowed. If the rate is zero, the payment is P divided by n. The formula assumes the rate and payment interval stay constant and instalments are monthly. Rounding, payment dates and lender conventions can produce small differences from a statement.

Worked example: compare two tenures

Assume a hypothetical principal of ₹10,00,000, a constant annual rate of 12% and regular monthly instalments. The example excludes processing fees, insurance, collateral or guarantee costs, taxes on charges, late fees and any rate reset.

This is a mathematical comparison under fixed assumptions, not a recommendation about which term to choose. A real quotation may include charges or a different repayment structure.

Compare the full borrowing cost, not just EMI

Before comparing offers, identify whether the rate is fixed, floating or subject to a reset. Check repayment dates, any moratorium, the amount actually disbursed, fees and prepayment conditions. If the facility has irregular instalments or multiple disbursements, request a lender-generated schedule instead of forcing it into a standard EMI model.

The Reserve Bank of India's 15 April 2024 Key Facts Statement (KFS) instructions cover retail and micro, small and medium enterprise (MSME) term-loan products extended by regulated entities. The circular defines APR as the annual cost of credit, including interest and other charges associated with the credit facility, and describes the KFS and repayment schedule. Check the circular's scope and the documents for the specific facility; not every working-capital product is a standard term loan.

Frequently asked questions

Does the EMI include processing fees?

No. The calculator estimates principal and interest from the inputs. Fees and other charges must be reviewed separately and may affect APR and total cost.

Can I use the formula for a cash-credit or overdraft facility?

Not as a complete repayment model. Those facilities may use a different interest and repayment structure. Use the lender's statement or a schedule designed for that facility.

Does a lower EMI mean a lower total cost?

Not necessarily. A longer tenure can reduce the monthly instalment while increasing total interest, as the example shows.

Does this calculator confirm business-loan eligibility?

No. It does not assess credit history, turnover, cash flow, collateral, lender policy or scheme eligibility.
