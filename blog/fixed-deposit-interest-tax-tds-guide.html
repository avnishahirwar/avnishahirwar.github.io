Fixed Deposit Interest: Compounding, Tax and TDS Explained

Understand cumulative and non-cumulative deposits, interest compounding and tax withholding. · Published 13 November 2026

A fixed deposit (FD) pays interest according to the bank's product terms. The quoted rate, compounding frequency, payout choice, tenure and premature-closure rules all affect the amount received. The interest credited by the bank is not necessarily the same as the after-tax return.

1. Cumulative and non-cumulative deposits

In a cumulative FD, interest is accumulated and paid with principal at maturity, using the bank's compounding convention. In a non-cumulative FD, interest is paid at selected intervals such as monthly, quarterly or annually. The payout option changes cash flow and may change the effective yield.

2. Compounding frequency matters

For a simple annual-compounding illustration, maturity value is principal multiplied by (1 + annual rate)years. Banks may compound at different intervals and apply their own day-count and rounding rules, so the product's disclosure and deposit advice are the controlling sources.

Illustrative annual compounding: Maturity = Principal × (1 + rate)years

3. FD interest and income tax

FD interest is generally taxable under applicable income-tax rules and is usually reported as income in the relevant financial year. The final tax impact depends on the taxpayer's total income, tax regime, deductions and other circumstances. A bank's tax deduction at source (TDS) is a withholding mechanism; it is not necessarily the final tax liability.

4. What TDS means

Where statutory conditions and thresholds are met, the bank may deduct TDS on interest. The applicable threshold and rate can depend on the depositor's category, declarations and current law. Check the bank's TDS certificate, Form 26AS/AIS and official income-tax guidance. If TDS was deducted, it may be available as a tax credit subject to the return and tax records.

5. Compare the net outcome

Before choosing an FD, compare the interest payout schedule, compounding method, premature-withdrawal penalty, reinvestment terms and tax effect. Also check whether the institution and deposit are covered by DICGC insurance and understand its aggregate limit and eligibility rules.

6. Calculate and verify

Use the FD Calculator to estimate maturity or interest using the rate and compounding assumptions you enter. If you want to understand withholding, use the TDS Calculator as a separate estimate. For current tax rules, consult the Income Tax Department and your bank's deposit terms.

Reminder: TDS is not a final tax calculation. The taxpayer's actual liability is determined under the law using total taxable income and applicable credits.

7. Example: why tax withholding is not the final answer

Suppose an FD earns interest during a financial year and the bank deducts tax at source when the relevant statutory conditions are met. The bank's deduction reduces the amount paid or credited, but the depositor's final tax is calculated using the full taxable income and applicable rules. A person with other income, deductions or a different tax rate may have a final liability that is higher or lower than the amount withheld. Reconcile the bank's interest certificate with the annual information statement and tax return records.

8. Questions to ask before opening or renewing an FD

  • Is the quoted rate applicable to the selected tenure and deposit amount?
  • Is the deposit cumulative or does it pay interest periodically?
  • How does the bank calculate interest for a broken period or premature closure?
  • Can the rate change after renewal, and is auto-renewal enabled?
  • What tax documents will the bank issue?
  • Is the bank covered by DICGC and how does the aggregate insurance limit apply?

For deposit insurance details, consult the Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation. For tax reporting and withholding rules, use the Income Tax Department portal. The bank's deposit advice and terms should be checked before relying on a maturity estimate.

Frequently asked questions

Is FD interest tax-free?

FD interest is generally taxable, subject to the applicable law and any specific exemption. Check the current rules for your circumstances.

Does TDS equal my final tax?

No. TDS is tax withheld at source. Your final liability depends on total income, deductions, tax regime and credits.

Does monthly interest payout earn the same as cumulative compounding?

Not necessarily. Payout frequency and reinvestment affect cash flow and effective return. Compare the bank's actual product terms.
