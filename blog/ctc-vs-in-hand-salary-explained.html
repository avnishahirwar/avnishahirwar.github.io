CTC vs In-Hand Salary: Understand Your Salary Structure

Learn why employer CTC differs from monthly take-home pay and which components to check. · Published 13 November 2026

Cost to Company (CTC) is the employer's stated annual cost for employing a person. It is not the same as the amount credited to a bank account each month. CTC can include employer contributions, insurance premiums, variable pay and benefits that are not paid as monthly cash salary.

1. Know the three salary terms

CTC: the employer's total stated cost, which may include cash and non-cash components. Gross salary: salary before employee-side deductions and tax withholding, as defined by the employer's payslip structure. Net or in-hand salary: the amount payable after applicable deductions and withholding for the pay period.

2. Identify components that may not be monthly cash

  • Employer provident-fund contributions.
  • Gratuity provision included in CTC.
  • Group medical or life insurance premiums.
  • Annual or performance-linked variable pay.
  • Joining, retention or one-time bonuses subject to conditions.

3. Understand employee-side deductions

Common deductions may include employee provident-fund contributions, professional tax where applicable, income-tax TDS, insurance premiums or other authorised deductions. The exact components depend on the employer, state, salary structure and employee declarations.

4. Ask for the monthly and annual breakdown

When comparing job offers, request the fixed cash component, variable-pay conditions, employer and employee retirement contributions, insurance benefits, bonus payment dates, probation terms and expected monthly deductions. Compare guaranteed cash separately from conditional or non-cash benefits.

5. Worked comparison method

Start with annual CTC, remove components that are not monthly cash, convert the fixed cash components to a monthly gross figure, then subtract employee deductions and estimated tax withholding. This produces an approximate monthly take-home amount. It is only an estimate until the employer confirms the payroll calculation.

Approximate monthly take-home = monthly cash gross − employee deductions − estimated tax withholding

Frequently asked questions

Is CTC the same as annual salary?

Not necessarily. CTC may include employer contributions, insurance, gratuity provisions and variable benefits in addition to cash salary.

Why is in-hand salary lower than gross salary?

Employee contributions, tax withholding and other authorised deductions reduce the amount paid to the employee.

Should I compare job offers only by CTC?

No. Compare fixed monthly cash, variable-pay conditions, benefits, deductions, role expectations and payment terms.
