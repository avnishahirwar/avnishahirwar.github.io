How to Reduce Your EMI: Options to Review Before Changing a Loan

Compare rate revisions, tenure changes, prepayments and balance transfers with the full borrowing cost in view. · Published 13 November 2026

A monthly EMI can feel difficult when income changes or household costs rise. There is no single adjustment that is best for every borrower: a lower EMI can increase the total interest paid if the repayment period becomes longer. Before changing a loan, compare both the monthly cash-flow effect and the full cost over the remaining tenure.

1. Ask whether the interest rate can be revised

For a floating-rate loan, ask the lender for the current benchmark, spread, reset date and applicable conversion or switching fee. A lower rate can reduce the EMI or shorten the tenure, depending on the lender's recalculation. Compare the revised rate with any fee and the time remaining on the loan. A small rate reduction may have limited value if the loan is close to maturity.

2. Compare a longer tenure with its interest cost

Extending the repayment period usually lowers the required monthly instalment because the principal is spread over more months. However, interest accrues for longer. Ask the lender for a revised amortisation schedule and compare the total remaining interest—not just the new EMI.

3. Consider a part-prepayment

If you have surplus funds after keeping an appropriate cash reserve, a part-prepayment can reduce principal. Ask whether the lender will reduce the EMI, shorten the tenure or let you choose. Keeping the EMI unchanged and reducing tenure can save more interest in many standard amortising-loan examples, but the result depends on rate changes, payment date and loan terms.

4. Review refinancing or balance transfer offers

Compare the new lender's interest rate, processing fee, legal or valuation costs, insurance conditions, reset terms and foreclosure requirements. Calculate the break-even period: how long it takes for expected interest savings to exceed switching costs. Do not move a loan solely because an advertised rate looks lower.

5. Avoid common EMI traps

  • Do not ignore processing, conversion or foreclosure fees.
  • Do not compare loans with different remaining tenures as if they were identical.
  • Do not use every available rupee for prepayment if it leaves no cash buffer.
  • Do not assume a lower EMI means a cheaper loan.

Use the Loan EMI Calculator to compare principal, rate and tenure scenarios. For an extra principal payment, use the Loan Prepayment Calculator. Treat the result as an illustration and request the lender's written revised schedule before making a decision.

Questions to ask your lender

  1. What is the outstanding principal and current rate?
  2. What fee applies to a rate conversion or balance transfer?
  3. Can I choose lower EMI or shorter tenure after prepayment?
  4. Can you provide the revised amortisation schedule in writing?

7. Worked comparison: monthly relief versus total cost

Imagine a borrower has a remaining principal balance and is offered two options: keep the current tenure with a lower interest rate, or extend the tenure to reduce the monthly payment. The first option may lower both the monthly instalment and total remaining interest if the rate reduction is large enough and fees are modest. The second option can make the monthly budget easier but may increase the number of interest-bearing months. Ask the lender to provide both schedules with the same outstanding principal and the same comparison date.

When reviewing the schedules, compare the total of future instalments, any one-time charges and the date on which the loan would finish. If you are considering prepayment, also compare the cash left in your savings account after the payment. A lower debt balance is useful, but keeping enough money accessible for predictable expenses and unexpected costs is also important.

8. A simple decision checklist

Before accepting a revised loan offer, write down the existing rate, remaining tenure, outstanding balance, monthly EMI and total remaining interest. Add the proposed rate, new EMI, revised tenure and every fee. Confirm whether the rate is fixed or floating, how often it resets and whether the lender changes the spread. If the numbers are not supplied in writing, ask for them before agreeing. The Reserve Bank of India publishes lender and borrower information at RBI's official website; the loan agreement and lender schedule remain essential for the individual account.

Frequently asked questions

Does a lower EMI always save money?

No. A lower EMI achieved by extending tenure can increase total interest. Compare total remaining repayment and fees.

Is prepayment always better than investing surplus cash?

Not automatically. Compare the loan's effective cost, liquidity needs, risk tolerance, taxes and alternative uses of the funds.

Can a lender change my floating rate?

The lender applies the loan agreement's benchmark and reset terms. Ask for the current benchmark, spread and next reset date.
