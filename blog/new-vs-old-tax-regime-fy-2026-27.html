New vs Old Tax Regime FY 2026-27: Which Saves More?

The new regime keeps the FY 2025-26 slab structure in FY 2026-27, with a full rebate on taxable income up to ₹12,00,000 and a ₹75,000 standard deduction for salaried employees. The old regime can still win when deductions are large. Here is how to compare, with worked examples.

Under Clause 156 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, tax is fully rebated for taxable income up to ₹12,00,000, so a salaried employee earning up to about ₹12,75,000 gross pays no tax after the ₹75,000 standard deduction. Surcharge applies above ₹50 lakh and is not shown here.

Worked example: ₹15,00,000 gross salary

New regime: taxable income is ₹15,00,000 − ₹75,000 = ₹14,25,000. Tax = 5% of ₹4,00,000 (₹20,000) + 10% of ₹4,00,000 (₹40,000) + 15% of ₹2,25,000 (₹33,750) = ₹93,750 before cess. Four per cent health and education cess takes this to about ₹97,500. With no further deductions, the old regime on the same taxable income of ₹15,00,000 yields ₹1,50,000 plus cess at the FY 2026-27 old-regime structure, so the new regime is cheaper here even before deductions are added back.

Old regime: if the same person claims substantial Section 80C, 80D and home-loan interest deductions (₹3,50,000 combined), taxable income falls to ₹11,50,000. The comparison then depends on the remaining slab arithmetic against the new regime's rebate and lower rates. Compute both scenarios with your own deduction figures before deciding.

When the old regime can win

  • High home-loan interest under Section 24(b) on a self-occupied property.
  • Large, genuine Section 80C and 80D claims plus HRA exemption in a rent-heavy city.
  • Family with NPS employer contribution (80CCD(2)) and multiple exemptions together.

When deductions fall below roughly ₹4–5 lakh, the new regime usually costs less, mainly because of the ₹12 lakh rebate and lower slab rates.

Limitations and checks

This guide is an arithmetic illustration for resident individuals without surcharge or special incomes. Confirm current slabs and rebate provisions from official Income Tax Department rates, and treat NPS, employer contributions and capital gains separately, because they follow different rules. It is not personalised tax advice.
