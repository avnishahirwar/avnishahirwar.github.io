HRA Exemption: How to Estimate the Eligible Amount

Review the salary, rent and city inputs used in a house-rent allowance estimate. · Published 13 November 2026

House Rent Allowance (HRA) is a salary component that may qualify for a tax exemption when the applicable conditions are met. The amount exempt is not automatically equal to the HRA received or the rent paid. It is determined using the relevant salary, rent, city and statutory rules for the financial year.

1. Identify the inputs

  • HRA received from the employer for the relevant period.
  • Rent actually paid for the residence.
  • Salary components included in the statutory definition of salary for this calculation.
  • Whether the residence is in a metro city for the applicable rule.
  • The period for which HRA and rent were both applicable.

2. Understand the three-part comparison

Under the commonly used HRA exemption method, the exempt amount is the least of: actual HRA received; rent paid minus 10% of qualifying salary; and 50% of qualifying salary for specified metro cities or 40% for other locations. The precise salary definition and eligibility conditions must be checked for the applicable tax year.

Illustrative framework: Exemption = least of (HRA received; rent − 10% of qualifying salary; 50% or 40% of qualifying salary)

3. Keep records

Keep rent receipts and payment evidence. Depending on the employer's process and applicable rules, a landlord's PAN may be required above a specified annual rent threshold. If you live in a home owned by a spouse or relative, or pay rent to a related person, verify the legal and factual requirements rather than assuming the exemption applies.

4. Compare tax regimes

HRA exemption availability depends on the tax regime and current-year provisions. Do not assume that the same exemption is available in both regimes. Compare the overall tax position, not only the HRA line item, using consistent income and deduction inputs.

5. Estimate and verify

Use the HRA Exemption Calculator to model qualifying amounts and review the result against your payslip and rent records. For the wider tax comparison, use the Income Tax Calculator. Confirm current rules with the Income Tax Department or a qualified tax professional.

Scope: A calculator can help organise inputs but cannot verify that rent was actually paid, determine the legal nature of an arrangement or replace supporting documents.

6. Worked illustration with changing inputs

Assume a salaried employee receives HRA of ₹18,000 per month, pays monthly rent of ₹15,000 and has qualifying salary of ₹30,000 per month for the relevant HRA calculation. The three-part comparison would consider the HRA received, rent less 10% of qualifying salary, and the applicable 40% or 50% salary limit. In this illustration, rent less 10% of salary is ₹12,000. The eligible exemption is the lowest of the three amounts, subject to the actual legal definition of salary, location category, period and tax regime. This example is only to explain the comparison method.

7. Part-year changes need careful treatment

If you changed jobs, moved cities, changed rent or received HRA for only part of the year, do not multiply one month's result by 12 without checking the dates. Calculate each relevant period separately where the salary or rent inputs changed. A move between locations may change the applicable city percentage. Employer payroll may also use a different reconciliation method during the year, so compare the estimate with Form 16 and the final return data.

8. Documents and official verification

Keep rent receipts, bank transfer records or other proof of payment, the rental agreement where applicable, salary slips and the employer's HRA statement. Verify current conditions and documentation requirements on the Income Tax Department website. A calculator can perform arithmetic on supplied figures but cannot confirm that a claim is legally eligible or that documents are sufficient.

Frequently asked questions

Is the full rent amount exempt?

No. The exemption is calculated using a statutory comparison and is subject to qualifying salary, HRA received and actual rent paid.

Can I claim HRA if I own a house?

It depends on the facts, work location, actual residence and applicable tax rules. Review the circumstances with a qualified tax professional.

Is HRA exemption available under every tax regime?

No. Availability depends on the regime and current provisions. Verify the applicable financial-year rules.
