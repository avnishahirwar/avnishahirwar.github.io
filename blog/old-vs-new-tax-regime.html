Old vs New Tax Regime: Which One May Be Better?

A practical comparison guide for Indian taxpayers · FY 2026–27

There is no single tax regime that is best for everyone. The right comparison depends on your taxable income, salary structure, eligible deductions, exemptions, other income and the tax rules applicable to the year. The fastest way to compare is to calculate both regimes with the same verified information.

Important: Tax rules and forms can change. This guide explains a comparison method; it is not a personal tax recommendation. Confirm current provisions with the Income Tax Department before filing.

1. Understand the main difference

The new regime generally uses revised slab rates with fewer deductions and exemptions. The old regime may allow eligible deductions and exemptions, but its slab structure differs. A lower headline rate does not automatically mean a lower final tax bill: deductions, rebates, cess and special-rate income can change the outcome.

2. What to collect before comparing

  • Annual salary and taxable allowances
  • Standard deduction and other eligible salary adjustments
  • Eligible deductions or exemptions you can actually document under the old regime
  • Interest, rental, capital-gains or other income, where relevant
  • Age and residential status where they affect the applicable calculation

3. Compare on a like-for-like basis

Slab structureDifferent slab rates and thresholdsRevised slab structure

4. A simple worked method

Suppose a salaried person has gross salary, eligible deductions and a small amount of other income. First calculate taxable income under each regime using only deductions permitted by that regime. Then calculate tax, apply any eligible rebate and add applicable cess. Compare the final payable amounts—not just the slab percentages.

6. Common mistakes to avoid

  • Comparing gross salary in one regime with taxable income in the other.
  • Counting a deduction without checking eligibility and proof requirements.
  • Forgetting other income, rebate conditions or cess.
  • Using a calculator configured for a different financial or assessment year.
  • Treating an estimate as an ITR filing result.

Frequently asked questions

Is the new tax regime always better?

No. The result depends on income composition, eligible deductions and exemptions, special-rate income, rebates and the applicable year. Compare both regimes using the same verified inputs.

Can I claim deductions under the old regime?

Eligible deductions and exemptions may be available under the old regime, subject to the law and documentation for the relevant tax year.

Is the new regime the default?

The new regime has generally been the default for eligible individual taxpayers, while opting for the old regime depends on the applicable rules and filing conditions. Verify the current year’s requirements on the Income Tax Department portal.

Does this calculator file my tax return?

No. A calculator provides an estimate only; it does not prepare or file an income-tax return.
