RBI Loan Key Facts Statement: 7 Things to Check Before Signing

By Avnish Ahirwar, Founder and Editor · Published 13 November 2026, 9:53 PM IST · Educational explainer

Two loan offers can advertise similar interest rates and still have different overall costs. Processing fees, other lender charges, the repayment period and the way interest is calculated can change what a borrower ultimately pays. That is why the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) introduced a standardised Key Facts Statement (KFS) framework for specified new retail and micro, small and medium enterprise (MSME) term loans.

The RBI circular dated 15 April 2024 says the KFS framework applies to new retail and MSME term loans sanctioned on or after 1 October 2024 by the regulated entities listed in the circular. It requires a prospective borrower to receive key loan information in a standardised format before executing the loan contract. Credit-card receivables are excluded from this circular. This guide explains how to read the document and what to compare; it is not a report of a newly announced RBI rule.

Quick takeaway: Do not compare loans using the headline interest rate or EMI alone. Read the APR, the fee breakdown, the repayment schedule, the validity period and the exact terms in the loan agreement.

1. Check the APR, not only the advertised interest rate

The interest rate is the price of borrowing the principal, but it may not capture every charge connected with the loan. The KFS includes an Annual Percentage Rate (APR) computation sheet. Under the RBI framework, APR represents the annualised cost of credit and includes charges levied by the regulated entity. Read the calculation sheet and ask the lender to explain any item you do not understand.

APR helps make offers more comparable, but you should still check the amount, timing and recipient of each charge. Where a third-party service is involved, confirm how that charge is disclosed and whether it is optional or required for the offer.

2. Read the complete list of fees and charges

Look for the processing fee and any other charge shown in the KFS. Ask whether a quoted fee is a one-time payment, recurring payment or a deduction from the amount disbursed. If a lender mentions an insurance premium, documentation cost or service fee separately, ask for the written basis and whether it is included in the APR calculation or collected on behalf of another provider.

Never rely only on a verbal explanation. Ask for the fee amount, purpose, timing and refund conditions in writing before you accept the offer.

3. Check the repayment or amortisation schedule

The KFS includes the repayment schedule for the loan over its tenor. Review the number of instalments, due dates, instalment amount and how the outstanding balance changes. In an amortising loan, each regular payment generally contains an interest component and a principal component. The split changes over time, so the schedule is more informative than a single EMI figure.

4. Note the KFS validity period

The RBI circular provides a minimum validity period for the KFS: at least three working days for loans with a tenor of seven days or more, and one working day for loans with a tenor shorter than seven days. This gives a prospective borrower a defined period to consider the disclosed terms. The circular also states that if the borrower agrees within the validity period, the regulated entity is bound by the terms stated in the KFS.

Check the proposal number and the date on the document. If the offer changes, request an updated KFS and compare the revised terms rather than assuming an earlier quote still applies.

5. Make sure you understand the language and every condition

The KFS is to be written in a language understood by the borrower. The lender must explain its contents and obtain an acknowledgement that the borrower has understood them. If a term is unclear, ask for a plain-language explanation before signing. Pay particular attention to fixed or floating interest, reset dates, late-payment charges, prepayment conditions and events that can change the repayment amount.

Keep the loan amount and comparison period consistent. A lower EMI can simply reflect a longer tenor, which may increase the total interest paid. The right comparison is the complete cost and repayment obligation, not one attractive number.

7. Keep the KFS and agreement together

Save the KFS, the final loan agreement, the repayment schedule and written clarifications from the lender. Check that the final agreement matches the offer you accepted. If any amount, fee or condition differs, ask the lender to explain the difference before proceeding. For questions or complaints, first use the lender's official grievance channel and retain the reference number and documents.

What the RBI rule does—and does not—mean

The KFS framework improves standardisation and disclosure for the loans covered by the circular. It does not mean that every loan is automatically low-cost, that every fee is waived, or that the lender must approve an application. It also does not replace the need to read the full agreement, check affordability or understand the consequences of late payments.

For exact scope and wording, read the RBI circular: Key Facts Statement (KFS) for Loans & Advances, 15 April 2024. The circular lists the regulated entities it covers and sets out the standardised disclosure requirements.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Key Facts Statement for a loan?

It is a standardised summary of key loan terms, including the APR computation and repayment schedule, provided to help a prospective borrower understand the offer before signing.

Does APR include loan charges?

Under the RBI KFS framework, APR includes charges levied by the regulated entity and disclosed in the KFS. Check the document for the detailed breakdown and any third-party charges collected through the lender.

Which loans are covered by the RBI KFS directions?

The 15 April 2024 RBI circular applies to new retail and MSME term loans sanctioned on or after 1 October 2024 by the regulated entities listed in the circular. Credit-card receivables are excluded from these directions.

Can I compare two loans using EMI alone?

No. Compare APR, total amount payable, fees, tenor, rate type, reset conditions and repayment schedule as well as EMI.

Is this article a loan recommendation?

No. It is general educational information. Review the actual KFS and agreement and consult a qualified professional when needed.
