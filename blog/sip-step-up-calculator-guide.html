SIP Calculator with Step Up: How Annual Increases Change a Projection

India-focused guide · Reviewed 14 November 2026

A SIP calculator with step up estimates how a recurring monthly contribution may change when the contribution is increased at a chosen interval, commonly once a year. It is a mathematical illustration based on inputs—not a prediction of mutual-fund performance.

What does a step-up SIP calculator calculate?

A regular SIP projection keeps the monthly contribution constant. A step-up SIP projection increases the contribution by a chosen percentage or amount at each annual step. This lets a reader compare two contribution schedules while keeping the assumed return and investment period visible.

How the step-up SIP calculation works

For each year, the calculator uses that year's monthly contribution and the number of instalments in the year. It applies the selected monthly growth assumption to each instalment until the end of the projection period, then adds the yearly future values.

Conceptual formula: Future value = sum of each instalment × its remaining monthly growth factors. Annual step-up contribution in year k = starting monthly SIP × (1 + step-up rate)k−1.

The exact result depends on whether contributions are modelled at the beginning or end of each month, how the annual rate is converted to a monthly rate, and when the annual step-up is applied. Check those assumptions on the calculator before comparing results.

Worked illustration

Suppose a hypothetical contribution starts at ₹2,000 per month and increases by 10% after each completed year. The second year's monthly contribution would be ₹2,200, the third year's ₹2,420, and so on. These amounts describe the contribution schedule only; the final corpus also depends on the assumed return and timing convention.

Step-up SIP versus a fixed SIP

A fixed SIP keeps the contribution unchanged. A step-up SIP raises it at the selected interval. The step-up scenario will usually show higher total contributions, so compare the projected value alongside the total amount contributed—not just the final corpus.

Step-up SIP versus a lump-sum illustration

A lump-sum model assumes one contribution at the start of the period. A SIP model assumes multiple contributions over time. These schedules have different cash-flow timing, so the outputs are not directly comparable unless the contribution amounts and dates are made equivalent.

Assumptions and limitations

  • The return rate is an input assumption, not a promised or expected return.
  • Market-linked returns vary; a constant-rate model does not simulate NAV changes.
  • Taxes, fund expenses, exit loads, missed instalments and inflation may be excluded.
  • A higher contribution schedule may not fit every household budget; this page does not recommend an investment amount or a fund.

Frequently asked questions

What is a step-up SIP?

It is a recurring investment schedule in which the contribution is increased at a chosen interval, often annually.

How is a 10% annual SIP step-up calculated?

The monthly contribution for each new year is 10% higher than the previous year's monthly contribution, if the calculator uses a percentage-based annual step-up.

Is a step-up SIP return guaranteed?

No. A calculator uses an assumed rate to illustrate a scenario. Mutual-fund returns and capital values can rise or fall.

Is step-up SIP better than lump sum?

They use different cash-flow timings. A calculator can compare mathematical scenarios, but it cannot determine which is suitable for a person's circumstances.
