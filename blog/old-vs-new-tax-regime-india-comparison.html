Old vs New Tax Regime in India: How to Compare Your Tax Estimate

Compare tax regimes with consistent salary, deduction and financial-year inputs. · Published 13 November 2026

Choosing between the old and new income-tax regimes is a comparison exercise, not a one-size-fits-all rule. The better result depends on your taxable income, eligible deductions and exemptions, salary structure, and the rules that apply to the financial year. A headline tax rate or a single deduction rarely tells the whole story.

1. Start with the correct financial year

Tax slabs, rebates, surcharge rules, deductions and filing provisions can change. First identify the financial year for the income being assessed and use the official rules for that year. Do not copy slab values from an older article or a previous return. The Income Tax Department's portal and current Finance Act materials should be the reference for rates and eligibility.

2. Understand the broad difference

The old regime generally allows a wider set of specified deductions and exemptions when the taxpayer meets the conditions. The new regime generally uses a different slab structure and restricts or removes many deductions and exemptions. Which structure produces a lower tax estimate depends on the actual numbers—not merely whether a person has a home loan, insurance premium or rent payment.

3. Gather the same inputs for both calculations

  • Annual salary and taxable allowances.
  • Standard deduction and any other deduction permitted under the chosen regime.
  • Eligible HRA or other exemptions, if applicable.
  • Eligible Chapter VI-A deductions where the selected regime permits them.
  • Other income, including interest and rental income.
  • Age, residential status and other details required by the current rules.

Use the same income figures in both scenarios. If you compare one regime with a complete salary estimate and the other with only basic pay, the result will be misleading.

4. Do not assume every payment is deductible

Premiums, provident-fund contributions, housing-loan interest, rent and education-loan interest have specific eligibility conditions and limits. Some benefits may be unavailable under the new regime or may require supporting documents. A payment made during the year does not automatically qualify for a tax deduction. Verify the current provision and the taxpayer's eligibility before entering it into a comparison.

5. Include income beyond salary

Bank interest, fixed-deposit interest, capital gains, rental income and other taxable receipts can change the result. Some income categories may have special tax treatment. A simple salary-only estimate is not a complete return calculation. If your income includes complex capital gains, business income, foreign assets or special-rate income, get the relevant details reviewed separately.

6. Run two scenarios and compare the final tax

Use the Income Tax Calculator with the correct financial-year settings. Calculate the old-regime result using only eligible deductions and exemptions. Then calculate the new-regime result with its applicable rules. Compare the estimated tax payable after applicable rebate, cess and surcharge—not just the slab rate or taxable income.

7. Example of the comparison method

Imagine a salaried person has annual income and several possible deductions. Under the old regime, the person enters only the deductions that are actually eligible and supported. Under the new regime, the person removes deductions that are not available and uses the applicable standard deduction and slab rules. The calculator produces two separate estimates. The difference between those estimates is the starting point for a decision; it is not a substitute for checking declarations, employer payroll treatment and the final return.

8. Review HRA and housing-related inputs carefully

If you receive HRA, first check whether the exemption is available under the selected regime and whether the rent and salary inputs meet the relevant definition. The HRA Exemption Calculator can help organise an eligible HRA estimate, but it cannot validate rent receipts, landlord details or legal eligibility. Do not enter an HRA exemption into a regime where the law does not permit it.

9. Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using tax slabs from the wrong financial year.
  • Claiming a deduction without checking the applicable regime.
  • Forgetting interest or other income outside salary.
  • Comparing gross salary in one scenario with taxable salary in the other.
  • Treating TDS as the final tax liability.
  • Assuming the calculator has reviewed supporting documents.

10. Verify before filing

Check the latest guidance on the Income Tax Department portal, compare the calculation with Form 16 and other income records, and confirm the selected regime within the applicable deadline and filing process. If the case involves multiple income sources, unusual exemptions or a significant tax impact, consult a qualified tax professional. A calculator is a planning aid, not an official assessment.

Frequently asked questions

Which tax regime is better?

It depends on the financial year, taxable income and eligible deductions or exemptions. Compare both using the same complete inputs.

Can I claim HRA in both regimes?

HRA exemption availability differs by regime. Check the current financial-year rules before including it.

Does a tax calculator guarantee the final tax?

No. It estimates from supplied inputs and assumptions. The return, supporting documents and current law determine the final liability.
