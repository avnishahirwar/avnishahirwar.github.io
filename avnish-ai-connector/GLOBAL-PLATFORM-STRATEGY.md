# Avnish AI — Global Platform Strategy
Version: 1.0
Status: strategic proposal only. This is not a launched company, approved brand, operating service, or revenue forecast.

## 1. Brand decision

Working brand: **AVNISH AI**
Descriptor: **Global AI, Technology & Everyday Solutions**
Proposed promise: **Make useful technology easier for everyone.**

Keep AVNISH AI as the working name because it continues the existing project identity. Do not register, print, advertise at scale, or claim exclusive rights until professional trademark clearance is completed in India and target markets, including relevant software, SaaS, education, business, and service classes. Domain and social-handle availability must also be checked independently. A quick web search is not legal clearance.

## 2. Ambition and operating principle

Build a global technology group that serves individuals, creators, small businesses, and enterprises. The long-term portfolio may contain many products and revenue streams, but it must be built as a portfolio of focused products sharing secure infrastructure—not 100 unrelated businesses launched simultaneously.

The company should earn trust through useful outcomes, transparent pricing, reliable service, privacy, accessibility, and human support.

## 3. Portfolio architecture: 10 divisions

1. **AI Personal Productivity** — personal knowledge assistant, task planning, document summarization, language support, accessibility helpers.
2. **AI Business Operations** — enquiry management, workflow automation, customer support drafts, CRM helpers, reporting.

4. **Marketing & Creator Tools** — content calendars, approved social analytics, brand asset workflows, campaign reporting, creator-business CRM.
5. **Commerce & Customer Experience** — product catalogues, customer help, order-status integrations, returns workflow, customer feedback.
6. **Hospitality & Travel Technology** — guest enquiry assistant, property FAQ, staff workflow, itinerary information, review analysis.
7. **Learning & Career Tools** — study planners, language practice, skills portfolios, interview preparation, course administration.
8. **Data & Decision Intelligence** — dashboards, data cleanup, operational reports, forecasting support with clear limitations.
9. **Cloud, API & Developer Platform** — secure APIs, usage-based developer tools, integration connectors, documentation, monitoring.
10. **Enterprise & Professional Services** — discovery, integration delivery, training, managed support, implementation projects.

These are future business lines, not currently available services. Any high-stakes professional advice (medical, legal, investment, tax) is excluded unless delivered by appropriately qualified professionals under applicable rules.

## 5. Revenue architecture

Potential revenue types:
- Monthly and annual SaaS subscriptions.
- One-time implementation and setup fees.
- Custom project fees with written scope and acceptance criteria.
- Metered API usage.
- Managed support retainers.
- Training and onboarding fees.
- Enterprise contracts.
- Optional partner/reseller arrangements with written agreements.

Avoid selling every service as a separate product at first. Bundle services around a validated customer problem and publish clear limits, support terms, refund policy, and service-level commitments.

## 6. First product: choose a narrow wedge

Recommended first product: **Avnish AI Business Desk** for enquiry-driven small businesses, starting with independent hotels and hospitality operators.

Core MVP:
- Approved business FAQ knowledge base.
- Enquiry capture with consent and clear privacy notice.
- Staff inbox and assignment.
- Suggested replies grounded in approved information.
- Human approval before external messages.
- Follow-up task reminders.
- Basic response-time and enquiry reports.

Why this first:
- A visible, recurring workflow.
- Clear business buyer and measurable operational outcomes.
- Can start with one business category and expand to other enquiry-driven industries.
- Can be sold first as a scoped setup service, then standardized as SaaS.

Do not claim it creates bookings or increases revenue until verified customer results support those claims.

## 7. Staged execution

### Stage A — Validate (weeks 1–2 indicative)
- Interview 10–15 prospective buyers and users.
- Identify the most expensive or frustrating repeated enquiry workflow.
- Validate willingness to pay and current alternatives.
- Secure 2–3 design partners with written pilot expectations.
- Define baseline metrics and a small product specification.

### Stage B — Prototype (weeks 3–6 indicative)
- Complete live MCP protocol verification of existing connector.
- Build a separate authenticated application prototype using test data.
- Implement FAQ management, enquiry inbox, consent, human approval, and audit trail.
- Test security boundaries and tenant isolation.
- Do not connect real private social accounts or payment rails yet.

### Stage C — Pilot (weeks 7–10 indicative)
- Pilot with 1–3 consenting properties.
- Measure response time, follow-up completion, staff adoption, and support burden.
- Fix issues and verify customers find the product useful.
- Convert only where the customer agrees to clear paid terms.

### Stage D — Billing and scale (weeks 11–16 indicative)
- Select a payment provider after legal account and product eligibility review.
- Complete sandbox checkout, webhook verification, subscription entitlements, refunds, cancellation, and reconciliation tests.
- Launch paid plans only after production security and compliance gates pass.
- Add another industry only after the first product demonstrates retention and support economics.

Timing is indicative, not guaranteed. Provider approval, legal setup, product complexity, and customer validation may change it.

## 8. Indicative pricing experiments

These are hypotheses to test, not current plans or market-validated prices:
- Individual productivity: free tier plus a modest paid tier.
- Small business SaaS: ₹999–₹4,999 per month depending on usage and staff seats.
- Hospitality workflow: ₹1,499–₹8,999 per property per month, plus setup for custom work.
- Custom implementation: fixed quote after discovery and written scope.
- API: usage-based with transparent quotas and overage controls.
- Enterprise: negotiated contract only after support and security capabilities exist.

For international pricing, set country-specific prices only after checking local purchasing power, tax, currency, payment availability, support costs, and competitor benchmarks.

## 10. Payments and account controls

Payment gateway is not connected or implemented today. Future design:
1. Customer chooses a plan.
2. Backend creates a hosted checkout session.
3. Payment provider processes payment.
4. Backend verifies signed webhooks and idempotency.
5. Subscription entitlements are activated server-side.
6. Invoice, cancellation, refund, and reconciliation flows are available.

Never store raw card details. A legal account owner must complete payment-provider onboarding, identity checks, business verification, tax setup, and bank settlement configuration. Any process requiring an adult or legal representative must be handled by an eligible adult; do not bypass age, identity, or platform rules.

## 11. Safety, privacy, and professional boundaries

- Minimize personal data collection and set retention periods.
- Provide consent, access, correction, deletion, and opt-out mechanisms as applicable.
- Use encryption, tenant isolation, role-based access, secrets management, audit logs, and backups.
- Keep private customer data out of the public MCP connector.
- Human review is required for consequential external communications and customer commitments.
- Do not offer medical, legal, financial, or tax advice as automated professional services.
- Use qualified professionals for regulated services and compliance.
- No fake engagement, spam, impersonation, deceptive reviews, or unauthorized scraping.

## 13. Decision framework

Prioritize each opportunity by:
1. Customer pain and willingness to pay.
2. Repeatability across customers.
3. Gross margin after AI, hosting, messaging, support, and payment costs.
4. Legal and provider feasibility.
5. Build and maintenance effort.
6. Retention potential.
7. Fit with shared platform capabilities.

Build only the highest-scoring product next. A portfolio of 100 revenue opportunities is a long-term map, not a 100-product launch commitment.
