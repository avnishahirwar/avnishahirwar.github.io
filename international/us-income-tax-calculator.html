2026 assumptions and effective dates

  • Federal standard deduction: $16,100 single/MFS, $32,200 joint, $24,150 head of household.
  • Federal income-tax brackets: progressive 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35% and 37% bands.
  • Employee Social Security: 6.2% on wages up to $184,500.
  • Employee Medicare: 1.45% on all modeled wages.
  • Additional Medicare Tax: 0.9% above $200,000 single/head of household, $250,000 married filing jointly or $125,000 married filing separately.
  • Rates apply to calendar tax year 1 January–31 December 2026.

This is a simplified federal estimate, not a full Form 1040 or payroll withholding calculation. State/local income taxes, tax credits, itemized deductions, dependants, pre-tax benefits, retirement contributions, other income and special adjustments are excluded. Additional Medicare Tax is estimated only from the wages entered here; spouse wages and self-employment income are not combined, so joint or mixed-income households may need a separate calculation.

Frequently asked questions

Which tax year does this US calculator use?

It uses 2026 federal income-tax brackets and standard deductions.

Does it include state income tax?

It estimates federal income tax, employee Social Security, Medicare and Additional Medicare Tax on the wages entered. It does not include state/local tax or combine spouse wages and self-employment income.

How is Additional Medicare Tax estimated?

The calculator applies 0.9% to the entered wages above $200,000 for single/head-of-household filers, $250,000 for married filing jointly, or $125,000 for married filing separately. It does not combine a spouse's wages or self-employment income, and is not a payroll withholding calculation. See the IRS rules before relying on a result.

Does it include tax credits or itemized deductions?

No. It uses the 2026 standard deduction and does not calculate credits, itemized deductions or special income adjustments.
