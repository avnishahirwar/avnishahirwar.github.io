How this estimate works

  • The standard repayment is calculated from the outstanding balance, annual rate and remaining term.
  • The overpayment scenario keeps that regular payment unchanged and adds your extra monthly amount to reduce the balance.
  • Interest is estimated monthly on the remaining balance; the final payment is adjusted to clear the balance.
  • Results are illustrative and assume the interest rate stays constant for the full remaining term.

Monthly, annual and one-off mortgage overpayments

A regular monthly overpayment increases each scheduled payment and reduces the balance more frequently. An annual lump-sum payment can produce a different result because the extra money reaches the balance at a different time. This calculator models a fixed extra amount every month; it does not model annual lump sums, irregular payments, offset accounts or payment holidays.

Before paying extra, check your lender's annual allowance, whether the allowance resets by calendar year or mortgage year, the payment deadline, and whether the lender reduces your term or recalculates your monthly payment. Fixed-rate products may impose an early repayment charge if you exceed the permitted allowance.

Check your mortgage terms

Some lenders limit how much you can overpay without an early repayment charge. Fixed-rate, tracker and variable-rate products may have different rules. Check your mortgage offer and lender before making additional payments.

This estimate does not include fees, changing interest rates, payment-date conventions, daily interest, offset accounts, payment holidays, tax effects or lender-specific overpayment allocation. It assumes overpayments reduce the term rather than recalculate the regular payment.

Frequently asked questions

How are overpayments modelled?

The calculator assumes a fixed interest rate and regular monthly overpayments that reduce the outstanding balance and shorten the repayment term.

Does it include early repayment charges?

No. Check your mortgage offer for overpayment allowances, early repayment charges and lender-specific rules.

Will my lender reduce my monthly payment?

This model keeps the regular scheduled payment unchanged and applies overpayments to shorten the term. Your lender may handle overpayments differently.

How much interest could a £150 monthly overpayment save?

In an illustration with a £200,000 balance, 4.5% constant annual rate and 20 years remaining, adding £150 each month saves about £18,243 in interest and clears the balance 38 months sooner. Actual lender calculations may differ.

Can I overpay my UK mortgage every month?

It depends on your mortgage product and lender rules. Check your annual overpayment allowance, any early repayment charge and how your lender applies extra payments before making them.

Does an overpayment always shorten the mortgage term?

Not always. This calculator assumes the regular payment stays unchanged so extra payments shorten the estimated term. Some lenders instead recalculate the monthly payment or apply different rules.
