Formula and assumptions

This tool estimates a standard repayment mortgage using the loan amount, a constant annual interest rate and monthly payments over the selected term. The loan-to-value ratio is the mortgage amount divided by the property price.

  • Assumes the interest rate stays constant for the whole term.
  • Uses monthly amortisation; actual lender calculations may use different payment dates or rounding.
  • Does not calculate interest-only mortgages, fees, insurance, valuation costs, legal fees or early repayment charges.
  • Does not calculate Stamp Duty or assess affordability, eligibility or approval.

This example assumes the rate stays unchanged and excludes fees, insurance, taxes and lender-specific payment rules.

For a like-for-like comparison, keep the mortgage amount and annual rate the same, then change the term. How your deposit changes loan-to-value (LTV)

For a £300,000 property, the deposit changes the amount you need to borrow and therefore the LTV:

  • £30,000 deposit: £270,000 mortgage and 90% LTV.
  • £45,000 deposit: £255,000 mortgage and 85% LTV.
  • £60,000 deposit: £240,000 mortgage and 80% LTV.
  • £75,000 deposit: £225,000 mortgage and 75% LTV.
  • £90,000 deposit: £210,000 mortgage and 70% LTV.

LTV is one factor lenders may consider when pricing a mortgage. A lower LTV does not guarantee approval or a particular rate; affordability, credit history, property type and lender criteria also matter.

Repayment mortgage vs interest-only mortgage

This tool models a capital-and-interest repayment mortgage. Each scheduled payment covers the interest due and repays part of the original loan, so the balance is designed to reach zero by the end of the agreed term if the assumptions remain unchanged.

An interest-only mortgage generally charges interest during the term while the capital remains outstanding. It needs a separate, credible repayment plan for the capital. Do not use this calculator to estimate an interest-only payment: use your lender's illustration or a calculator designed for that mortgage type.

Before comparing products, check whether the quoted rate is fixed, tracker or variable, when it can change, the initial deal period, product fees and any early repayment charge. The rate entered above is held constant for the full term, so a real mortgage with rate changes can produce a different result.

How are UK mortgage repayments calculated?

A standard repayment mortgage uses a monthly payment that covers interest and gradually repays the capital. The estimate applies the monthly interest rate to the outstanding balance and spreads repayment over the selected number of months. A longer term generally lowers the scheduled monthly payment but can increase total interest when the rate is unchanged.

Loan-to-value (LTV) is the mortgage amount divided by the property purchase price, expressed as a percentage. Lenders may use LTV bands when setting product rates, but this calculator does not predict an offer or assess affordability.

Compare a regular repayment estimate with a separate UK mortgage overpayment calculator if you want to model additional monthly payments. Check MoneyHelper’s mortgage guidance and your lender’s illustration for fees, rate changes and eligibility.

Frequently asked questions

Is this a mortgage offer?

No. It is a repayment estimate using the rate you enter and does not check lender affordability or eligibility.

Does it include Stamp Duty?

No. Use the separate property-tax calculator for England and Northern Ireland SDLT; Scotland and Wales have different property transaction taxes.

What is loan-to-value?

Loan-to-value is the mortgage amount divided by the property price, expressed as a percentage.

How much is the monthly payment on a £200,000 mortgage at 4.5%?

For illustration, a £200,000 repayment mortgage at a constant 4.5% annual rate is about £1,111.66 per month over 25 years. The estimate excludes fees and is not a lender quote.

Does a longer mortgage term reduce the monthly payment?

A longer term usually reduces the scheduled monthly payment at the same rate and loan amount, but increases total interest because the balance is repaid over more months.

Does this calculator use live UK mortgage rates?

No. Enter the interest rate from the product illustration you want to model. The calculator does not fetch live lender rates or predict an offer.
