1. What are cookies?

Cookies are small text files that a website or a third-party service may store in your browser. Similar technologies include local storage, pixels, tags, SDKs and web beacons. They can support basic website functions, help understand aggregate usage or support advertising delivery and measurement.

2. Categories of cookies and similar technologies

Strictly necessary and functional technologies. These may support security, page delivery, load balancing, language preferences, accessibility or other features requested by a visitor. If a feature uses browser storage, its exact name and lifetime may depend on the browser and the feature. The public calculator pages are designed to perform calculations in your browser; calculator values are not intended to be stored in advertising cookies.

Analytics technologies. Google Analytics 4 (GA4) is the analytics service currently configured in the website code. Google Analytics may use cookies or similar identifiers to understand page views, device and browser information, approximate location, traffic sources and interactions. Analytics configuration can change; this page does not guarantee that a particular cookie is present in every browser session.

Advertising technologies. Google AdSense publisher code is present in the website code. If Google approves the site and serves ads, Google and its advertising partners may use cookies or similar technologies for ad delivery, frequency capping, measurement, security and, where permitted, personalisation. The presence of publisher code alone does not mean that ads are approved or currently being served.

Third-party embedded services. If you interact with an embedded form, media player, social widget or other third-party feature, that provider may set or read its own cookies under its privacy and cookie terms. A provider's technologies are subject to that provider's configuration and your interaction with the feature.

3. Google Analytics and advertising cookies

Google may use first-party or third-party cookies and similar technologies when its analytics or advertising services operate on a page. Depending on settings, region and product configuration, advertising technologies may be associated with Google or doubleclick.net domains. Exact cookie names, purposes and retention periods can change and may differ between visitors. We do not publish a fixed cookie-name inventory because we have not verified a stable, browser-independent list for all pages and regions.

Google explains how AdSense uses cookies in its AdSense cookie guidance and describes its advertising technologies separately. You can review Google's Privacy Policy.

4. How to manage cookies

  • Browser controls: Most browsers allow you to view, block or delete cookies and clear site data. Use your browser's privacy or site-settings controls for instructions specific to your device.
  • Google advertising preferences: You can review available personalised-advertising settings at Google Ads Settings. These settings do not necessarily stop all advertising or all storage.
  • Industry choices: Visitors may review participating advertising-company choices at aboutads.info, where available.
  • Consent messages: Where a consent message is legally required, use the choices presented by the applicable consent interface. Do not assume that changing browser settings is a substitute for a required consent mechanism.

Blocking or deleting cookies may affect remembered preferences or some embedded features. Essential website delivery and security functions may continue to operate where permitted by law.

5. Visitors in the EEA, United Kingdom and Switzerland

Where applicable, visitors in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland must be provided with an appropriate consent mechanism for relevant advertising storage and personalisation. Google provides publisher tools for European regulations messages. The current Privacy Policy records that the Google message had been selected but was not yet published at its last review. Friendly Calculator will not claim that a Google-certified consent management platform is active until the message has been published, configured for the site and tested. Google's certification does not by itself establish compliance with every applicable privacy law.

For publisher requirements, see Google's CMP requirements and European regulations message guidance.

6. Personal information and calculator inputs

Calculator inputs are processed in the visitor's browser on the public calculator pages unless a page clearly explains otherwise. We do not intentionally send salary amounts, tax figures, loan amounts, BMI measurements, form contents, passwords or other sensitive values to analytics or advertising providers. Please do not enter sensitive personal information into third-party forms or services unless you have reviewed their privacy notices.

7. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy when website features, third-party services, advertising settings or applicable requirements change. The latest version will be published on this page with its revision date. This page is an operational explanation, not legal advice or a representation that a formal legal compliance audit has been completed.
