Business tools for pricing and operating decisions

This collection focuses on common small-business calculations: unit economics, promotional pricing, utility costs and trip expenses. These are building blocks for planning, not a substitute for a complete profit-and-loss statement or a reviewed business forecast. Start by defining the question you need to answer and checking that the inputs match the same product, period and tax basis.

Understand unit economics

Compare the selling price with the full cost of supplying a product or service. Include packaging, delivery, payment processing, marketplace commissions, returns and other variable expenses where relevant. A simple profit result may look positive while the business still has fixed overheads, payroll, rent or financing costs to cover. Track markup and margin separately because they use different denominators.

Price promotions carefully

Use the discount tool to estimate the sale price and customer saving. Sequential discounts are applied to the already-reduced price, so a 10% discount followed by another 10% reduction is a 19% total reduction, not 20%. Review coupon conditions, taxes, shipping and returns before publishing an offer.

Estimate operating expenses

Electricity and fuel calculations can support rough budgets. Utility bills may include slab tariffs, fixed charges, duties and adjustments; vehicle fuel use varies with route and conditions. Enter current local prices and reconcile estimates with actual invoices and bills.

When to seek a formal review

Use these calculators for quick comparisons and preliminary planning. Confirm tax treatment, statutory reporting, accounting entries and contractual obligations using current official sources or qualified professionals. Do not use an arithmetic estimate as the only basis for a major business commitment.
