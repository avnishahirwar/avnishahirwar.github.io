This collection helps you explore numbers behind a workout without presenting them as medical advice. Choose a tool, read its assumptions, and adjust the inputs to match the activity or training scenario you are considering. Results are estimates: actual energy use, pace, performance and recovery vary with technique, terrain, rest, equipment, experience, health and many other factors.

Safe and useful exercise planning

Start gradually, use an intensity that matches your experience, warm up when appropriate, and stop if you feel unwell. A calculator cannot assess movement quality, injury risk, cardiovascular status or whether an exercise is suitable for you. If you have a health condition, are pregnant or postpartum, are returning after an injury, or are unsure where to start, ask a qualified healthcare professional or appropriately certified exercise professional for individual guidance.

Do not treat a calorie estimate as permission to overtrain or as a precise food prescription. Do not compare one person’s strength estimate with another person’s capability. Hydration, nutrition, sleep and rest are also important. For younger users, a parent, guardian, teacher or qualified coach can help with age-appropriate activity planning.
