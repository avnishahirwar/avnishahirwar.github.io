How to use finance calculators responsibly

Financial calculators help compare scenarios; they do not predict market performance or replace the terms supplied by a bank, employer, government scheme or investment provider. Before calculating, confirm whether the amount is monthly, annual or one-time, whether the interest rate is nominal or effective, and whether the result assumes contributions at the beginning or end of a period.

Loan EMI and total borrowing cost

A standard reducing-balance EMI estimate uses the principal, monthly interest rate and number of instalments. Compare offers using the same principal and tenure. A smaller monthly instalment may simply reflect a longer repayment period and a higher total interest cost. Processing charges, insurance, rate resets, late fees and prepayment terms may sit outside the basic EMI formula; review the lender's written schedule before making a decision.

Fixed deposits, savings and provident funds

Fixed-deposit maturity estimates depend on the entered rate, compounding frequency, tenure and payout choice. Actual bank calculations can differ because of day-count conventions, premature closure, tax and product-specific rules. Savings-account estimates may use a simplified daily-balance method. EPF and PPF projections rely on stated contribution and rate assumptions; statutory limits, annual rate changes, contribution timing, withdrawals and eligibility can affect actual balances.

SIP and inflation scenarios

A SIP calculator illustrates what regular contributions could grow to under a constant assumed return. Mutual-fund returns are market-linked and are not guaranteed; the estimate does not forecast future performance or necessarily include fees and taxes. An inflation calculator shows how purchasing power changes under a chosen rate, not what prices will actually be in future years. Try several reasonable assumptions rather than treating one result as a forecast.

Worked example: compare a loan consistently

Suppose you compare two loan offers for the same principal. Keep the loan amount and tenure unchanged, enter each lender's annual rate, and compare monthly EMI, total interest and total repayment. Then separately account for processing fees, insurance and prepayment conditions. This makes the comparison more meaningful than looking at the EMI alone.

Sources and limitations

Use current product documents and official sources for rates and rules. Friendly Calculator does not provide loans, accept deposits, manage investments or guarantee returns. Review the methodology and assumptions and the specific calculator page before relying on any estimate. For a decision that affects your finances, consult the relevant provider or a qualified professional.
