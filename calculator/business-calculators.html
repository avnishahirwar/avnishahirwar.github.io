Choosing the right business calculator

Business calculations are most useful when every input is measured on the same basis. Before estimating a selling price or margin, decide whether your figures include GST, shipping, platform fees and other costs. A calculator can perform the arithmetic accurately while still producing a misleading business decision if important costs are omitted.

Profit, margin and discount calculations

Use the Profit & Loss Calculator to compare a product's cost and selling price. Remember that profit percentage on cost (markup) is different from gross margin on revenue. Use the Discount Calculator to estimate the reduction from a reference price and the resulting sale price. For sequential promotions, calculate each discount on the amount remaining after the previous reduction rather than simply adding percentages.

GST and operating-cost estimates

The GST Calculator can help separate an assumed tax amount from a base or inclusive price, but it cannot decide the correct tax classification or rate for a supply. Electricity and fuel tools help estimate operating expenses from entered consumption, distance, efficiency and prices. Actual utility tariffs, local fuel prices and business contracts may include additional charges.

Practical checklist for business owners

  • Keep purchase cost, selling price and tax treatment clearly separated.
  • Include payment, delivery, marketplace and packaging costs where relevant.
  • Use current supplier quotes and utility bills instead of outdated assumptions.
  • Compare scenarios with one input changed at a time.
  • Reconcile estimates with bookkeeping records before making financial decisions.

These tools are designed for quick planning and arithmetic checks. They do not replace accounting software, statutory financial statements, tax filings or professional advice. For business-critical decisions, verify the assumptions against contracts, invoices and official rules.
