What is a salary TDS calculator?

Estimate indicative salary tax and remaining monthly deduction. Employer payroll can include exemptions, perquisites, declarations, previous-employer income and current assessment-year rules not represented by a simple tool.

How to use this salary TDS calculator

  1. Enter annual taxable income after eligible adjustments and deductions.
  2. Select the new regime or the old regime for an individual below 60.
  3. Select Section 87A rebate eligibility.
  4. Enter tax already paid and payroll months remaining (1–12).
  5. Review the estimated annual tax, remaining balance and indicative monthly TDS.

Formula and calculation method

Remaining tax = estimated annual tax − TDS / tax already paid. Indicative monthly TDS = max(0, remaining tax) ÷ payroll months remaining.

Worked example

Illustration only: estimated annual tax ₹60,000 − tax already paid ₹12,000 = ₹48,000 remaining. If 6 payroll months remain, indicative monthly TDS = ₹48,000 ÷ 6 = ₹8,000. The ₹60,000 is an example input, not a tax calculation or statutory rate.

Important information

This is not an employer instruction or exact statutory calculation. The old-regime option assumes an individual below 60. The simplified model does not calculate deductions or exemptions, surcharge, special-rate income or every payroll adjustment. Enter annual taxable income after applicable adjustments, then confirm current rules and payroll inputs with the employer or a qualified tax professional.

Salary TDS calculator guide: estimate monthly deduction

Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) is a mechanism through which tax may be withheld when specified payments are made. For salary, an employer generally estimates annual taxable salary and deducts tax across the remaining salary payments, considering declarations and eligible evidence under applicable rules. This calculator is an indicative estimate, not a payroll-system replacement.

How monthly TDS is estimated

A simplified approach estimates annual taxable income, calculates indicative annual tax under the selected regime and relevant year, subtracts tax already deducted or paid, and spreads the remaining amount across relevant salary months. Actual payroll may change when salary, declarations, proofs, employment dates or rules change.

Why deductions can vary month to month

A bonus, salary revision, joining or leaving during the year, revised declarations, additional income or updated tax information can change the employer's annual projection. Payroll may adjust later deductions to reconcile the expected annual amount. One month's TDS is therefore not always annual tax divided by twelve.

Information to check

  • Current salary structure and taxable components.
  • Tax regime and correct financial-year rules.
  • Eligible exemptions or deductions and supporting proofs.
  • Other income disclosed to the employer, where relevant.
  • Tax already deducted by a previous employer or paid separately.

TDS is not necessarily final tax

TDS is a tax credit mechanism. Final liability depends on complete annual income and eligible claims. Withholding may be higher or lower than final liability, resulting in an additional payment or refund. Reconcile Form 16 with AIS, Form 26AS and other records.

Common errors and limitations

Using the wrong year, assuming every salary component is exempt, overlooking other income or mixing monthly and annual figures can distort an estimate. This tool does not access payroll records, validate proofs or file returns. Confirm current provisions with official guidance or a qualified tax professional.

Who should use this salary TDS calculator?

Employees and payroll reviewers can use it to understand an indicative monthly tax deduction from the annual salary-tax estimate, tax already paid and the number of payroll months remaining. It does not replace an employer's payroll computation.

Inputs explained

Enter the annual taxable salary figure and eligible adjustments described on the page, select the tax regime and rebate eligibility, add tax already paid, and enter the remaining payroll months. The output spreads the estimated remaining liability across the months entered; it does not verify payroll records.

Common mistakes and practical use

  • Entering gross salary when the field expects taxable income after the stated adjustments.
  • Forgetting TDS or advance tax already credited.
  • Using the wrong number of salary months remaining in the financial year.
  • Assuming the model includes every exemption, perquisite, previous-employer income or special-rate item.

Use the result to prepare a payroll query or compare an estimate with a payslip. Confirm the final computation with payroll records and official tax documents.
