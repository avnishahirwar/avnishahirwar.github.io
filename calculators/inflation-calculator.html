Inflation Calculator

Estimate how a price may change over time at an assumed annual inflation rate.

What this calculator does

Estimate a future cost by applying a constant annual inflation assumption. This is a scenario, not a forecast; actual inflation varies by category, location and time.

How to use it

  1. Enter current amount
  2. assumed annual inflation
  3. number of years
  4. review future cost.
Calculate now

Formula and calculation method

Future cost = current cost × (1 + annual inflation rate ÷ 100)^years.

Worked example

At an assumed 5% annual rate, ₹10,000 becomes approximately ₹12,763 after five years under a constant-rate model.

Guide: understanding your Inflation Calculator results

What the calculator shows

Inflation raises prices over time, so the same rupees buy less. At 6% a year, something that costs ₹50,000 today would cost about ₹89,542 after 10 years, and ₹50,000 saved today would have the purchasing power of only about ₹27,920 in today's terms. The formula is future cost = amount × (1 + rate)^years.

Using it to set savings targets

When you plan a goal such as school fees or a wedding in 10 years, use the future cost, not today's. Then work backwards with the SIP or FD calculator to see what monthly saving is needed. Education and medical costs have often risen faster than general inflation, so a higher rate may be more realistic for them.

The rule of 72

A quick check is to divide 72 by the inflation rate to estimate how many years it takes for prices to double. At 6% it is about 12 years, at 4% about 18 years.

Worked example with real numbers

₹50,000 at 6% inflation for 10 years: estimated future cost ₹89,542.38, additional amount ₹39,542.38, and today's ₹50,000 would be worth about ₹27,919.74 in purchasing power after 10 years.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming a single inflation rate for every kind of expense.
  • Comparing nominal returns without subtracting inflation.
  • Treating the estimate as a forecast.
  • Leaving idle cash in a low-interest account for years.

Important information

Different categories such as food, housing, transport and education can change at different rates. Check official inflation statistics for historical data.

Frequently asked questions about the Inflation Calculator

What inflation rate should I use?

India's retail inflation target is 4% with a tolerance band of 2 percentage points on either side. For long-term planning many people use 5–6%, and a higher rate for education and healthcare.

What is the real return on an FD?

Approximately the FD rate after tax minus inflation. A 7% FD taxed at 30% yields about 4.9%; with 6% inflation, the real return is below 0.

Is the calculation compounded monthly?

No. It compounds annually, which is the usual way to project multi-year costs.

Can I use it for salary growth?

Yes. Enter your salary and the expected annual rise to see what it becomes.

Why is the purchasing power lower than the original amount?

Because prices have risen. The tool divides today's amount by the cumulative price rise to show what it can buy.
