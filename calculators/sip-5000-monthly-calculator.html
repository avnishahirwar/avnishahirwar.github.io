What a SIP projection measures

A Systematic Investment Plan schedules recurring purchases in a mutual fund. This projection isolates the effect of contribution size, duration, assumed rate and instalment timing. It does not compare or rank mutual-fund schemes.

Formula and calculation method

Let P = monthly contribution, i = annual assumed return ÷ 12 ÷ 100, and n = number of monthly contributions. For end-of-month contributions: FV = P × [((1 + i)n − 1) ÷ i]. For beginning-of-month contributions: FV = P × [((1 + i)n − 1) ÷ i] × (1 + i). If i = 0, FV = P × n. The exponent n is applied to the full factor (1 + i).

Worked example

With a monthly contribution of ₹5,000, an assumed annual return of 12% compounded monthly and a 10-year period, total contributions are ₹6,00,000. If contributions are made at the end of each month, the illustrative future value is about ₹11,50,193 and the illustrative gain is about ₹5,50,193. Beginning-of-month contributions produce a different illustration because each payment is modelled as invested one month earlier. These are mathematical illustrations, not promised or guaranteed returns; actual mutual-fund values can be lower or higher.

What this projection leaves out

  • Changes in the fund's NAV between instalment dates.
  • Expense ratio, exit load and transaction costs.
  • Capital-gains tax and the investor's tax position.
  • Inflation and the future purchasing power of the projected amount.
  • Missed, paused, increased or irregular instalments.

These items can make an investor's realised value different from the mathematical projection.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the beginning-of-month estimate higher?

Each instalment is modelled as invested one month earlier, so the formula applies one additional monthly growth factor.

What does a 0% assumed rate show?

It shows only the sum of all instalments because the model applies no growth.

Does the calculator use an effective annual yield?

No. It treats the entered figure as a nominal annual rate and divides it by 12 to obtain the monthly rate.

Does the model use actual mutual-fund NAV data?

No. It applies a constant assumed monthly rate and does not simulate purchases at changing NAVs.

Are expense ratios and taxes included?

No. Fund expenses, exit loads, taxes and inflation are excluded from the displayed projection.

Does rupee-cost averaging prevent investment losses?

No. Regular purchases change the number of units bought at different NAVs, but they do not remove market risk or guarantee a positive return.

Why instalment timing changes the estimate

When the same amount is invested at the start rather than the end of a month, each instalment has one extra month in the model. The beginning-of-month result is therefore multiplied by one additional monthly growth factor. This timing difference is a feature of the arithmetic, not evidence that a particular investment date will produce a better market price.

Rupee-cost averaging is not a return formula

Regular purchases can result in buying more units when a fund's NAV is lower and fewer units when it is higher. The effect depends on the sequence of NAVs and does not remove the possibility of loss. The fixed-rate formula on this page does not model unit purchases at changing NAVs; it is a simplified future-value calculation.

Nominal rate and compounding convention

This calculator converts the entered nominal annual rate into a monthly rate by dividing it by 12. It then applies that monthly rate for each instalment period. If a source quotes an effective annual yield instead, dividing it by 12 is not the same conversion; convert the effective annual rate to an equivalent monthly rate before comparing like-for-like projections.

Choosing assumptions responsibly

Run several rates and durations to see how much the result depends on the assumptions. Check scheme documents for the fund category, benchmark, riskometer, costs and portfolio. Compare the projected amount with your goal's future cost after allowing for inflation.

Use the result to compare contribution and timing scenarios. It is not a fund-selection tool.

Calculation methodology & official sources

Who should use this SIP calculator?

This calculator helps readers understand how recurring monthly contributions and an assumed return affect a mathematical future-value projection. It is useful for learning and scenario comparison, not for predicting a mutual fund's actual performance.

Inputs explained

Monthly investment is the planned contribution. Assumed annual return is a hypothetical rate, not a guarantee. Investment period is the number of years. Contribution timing selects whether the model treats each instalment as made at the beginning or end of the month.

Common mistakes and real-life use

  • Treating an assumed return as a promised or stable market return.
  • Ignoring inflation, taxes, fund expenses and periods of negative returns.
  • Comparing projections that use different contribution dates or compounding conventions.

Use multiple return assumptions to see how sensitive the projection is, and read the scheme documents before making any investment decision.

₹5,000 monthly SIP: frequently asked questions

How much will I invest in 10 years at ₹5,000 per month?

You will contribute ₹6,00,000 over 120 monthly instalments, before returns, fees or taxes.

Is ₹11.5 lakh a guaranteed maturity value?

No. It is an illustration using a constant assumed monthly rate. Mutual-fund returns fluctuate and capital is at risk.

Can I change the 12% assumption?

Yes. The calculator is interactive. Try different rates and durations to compare mathematical scenarios.

Does this include inflation, tax or fund expenses?

No. Inflation, taxes, expense ratios, exit loads and transaction costs are excluded.

What if I invest at the beginning of each month?

The model adds one monthly growth period to each instalment compared with end-of-month timing. Actual transaction dates and NAVs differ.
