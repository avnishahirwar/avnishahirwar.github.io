What is a mutual fund calculator?

A mutual fund calculator estimates how an initial investment and recurring monthly contributions could grow under an assumed rate over a selected period. This tool combines a one-time lump-sum amount with a monthly Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) contribution. It helps explain compounding and the effect of regular contributions; it does not select, rank or recommend a mutual fund scheme.

Mutual fund values are market-linked and actual returns can be negative. The calculator uses a constant rate only to illustrate a scenario. It does not use live Net Asset Value (NAV) data, predict the market or guarantee a future value.

Formula and calculation method

The model converts the assumed annual percentage rate into a monthly rate by dividing it by 12 and 100. It assumes contributions are made at the end of each month and that the same monthly rate applies throughout the period.

Lump-sum future value: FV = P × (1 + i)n.

Monthly SIP future value: FV = S × [((1 + i)n − 1) ÷ i]. If i is zero, the SIP value is S × n.

P is the initial amount, S is the monthly contribution, i is the monthly rate as a decimal and n is the number of monthly periods. Total projected value is the lump-sum future value plus the SIP future value. Estimated gain or loss is projected value minus total contributions.

Worked example in Indian rupees

Suppose you enter an initial amount of ₹50,000, a monthly contribution of ₹2,000, an assumed annual rate of 10% and a period of 10 years. Total contributions are ₹50,000 + (₹2,000 × 120), or ₹2,90,000. Using a 10% nominal annual rate divided monthly, the modelled future value is approximately ₹5,45,042. The illustrative difference is about ₹2,55,042. These are formula outputs, not actual fund performance or a forecast.

How to use this calculator

  1. Enter an initial amount: use zero if modelling only monthly contributions, or enter the lump-sum amount you want to include.
  2. Enter a monthly SIP: enter the recurring contribution. Use zero if modelling only a lump sum.
  3. Choose an assumed annual return: test a hypothetical rate. It is an input for illustration, not an expected or assured result.
  4. Set the period and calculate: select a whole number of years, then review total contributions, projected value and the estimated difference.

Benefits and features

The calculator combines lump-sum and monthly SIP amounts. Change the inputs, use the sliders for quick scenarios, inspect the contribution-versus-growth bar, and download or share a summary of your assumptions. It reports contributions separately from the modelled value so the difference is clear.

What the estimate excludes

This projection does not include scheme expense ratios, exit loads, brokerage, transaction costs, taxes, inflation, missed contributions, withdrawals, switches, changing NAVs or changing rates. These factors can affect realised values. SIP instalments are modelled at month-end; beginning-of-month contributions would produce a different result.

Checks before interpreting a result

  • Compare several assumptions instead of relying on one rate.
  • Check whether the contribution and period are realistic for the scenario you are studying.
  • Review current scheme documents, risk information, expense ratio and exit-load terms separately.
  • Consider how inflation and taxes may affect future purchasing power.
  • Do not treat a higher assumed rate as evidence that a particular fund is suitable.

Mutual fund investments involve market risk. Read relevant scheme documents and official disclosures before making an investment decision. This calculation is educational, not personalised investment advice or a recommendation to buy or sell a fund.

Frequently asked questions

Does this calculator use actual mutual fund NAV data?

No. It applies a constant assumed monthly rate to your inputs. It does not fetch a fund's NAV history or simulate actual unit purchases.

Are the projected returns guaranteed?

No. The annual rate is a hypothetical input. Mutual fund returns are market-linked and can be higher or lower than any illustration, including negative.

Can I calculate only a SIP or only a lump-sum investment?

Yes. Set the initial amount to zero for monthly contributions only, or the monthly contribution to zero for a lump sum only. At least one amount must be greater than zero.

Are taxes and fund expenses included?

No. Expense ratios, exit loads, transaction costs, taxes and inflation are excluded. Review current scheme documents and applicable rules separately.

Why can my actual value differ from this projection?

Actual NAVs change, contributions may be missed or changed, and expenses, taxes, withdrawals and transaction dates affect outcomes. This model assumes a constant rate and month-end contributions.
