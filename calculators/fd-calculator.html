Formula and calculation method

Fixed deposit calculator guide: maturity and interest

A fixed deposit (FD) is a deposit held for a selected tenure under an agreed rate and payout structure. This calculator estimates maturity value or interest from the assumptions entered. The bank's deposit advice, product terms and final statement remain authoritative.

How cumulative FD maturity is estimated

A common compound-interest model is A = P × (1 + r/m)m×t, where P is principal, r is annual rate as a decimal, m is compounding periods per year and t is years. Interest earned is A − P. Actual institutions may use specific day counts, compounding conventions and rounding rules.

Cumulative versus periodic payout

A cumulative FD reinvests interest until maturity. A non-cumulative FD pays interest at selected intervals, such as monthly or quarterly, so the cash-flow pattern differs. Confirm which payout option the calculator models before comparing figures.

Example variables

Enter a hypothetical principal of ₹1,00,000, an assumed annual rate of 7% and a tenure of 2 years. Changing tenure or compounding frequency changes the projected value. The rate is an input assumption, not a live quote from a bank.

What to compare

  • Effective yield and interest payout frequency.
  • Premature withdrawal rules and penalties.
  • Auto-renewal and maturity payment instructions.
  • Tax treatment and any applicable TDS.
  • Institution status and applicable deposit-protection arrangements.

Tax and calculation limits

Interest may be taxable depending on current law and personal circumstances; TDS is not necessarily the final liability. Differences from a bank receipt may arise from actual deposit dates, leap years, day-count conventions, compounding and early closure. Use the estimate for planning and verify the final terms with the institution.
