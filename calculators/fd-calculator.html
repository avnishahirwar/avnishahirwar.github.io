Fixed deposit calculator guide: maturity and interest

A fixed deposit (FD) is a deposit held for a selected tenure under an agreed rate and payout structure. This calculator estimates maturity value or interest from the assumptions entered. The bank's deposit advice, product terms and final statement remain authoritative.

How cumulative FD maturity is estimated

A common compound-interest model is A = P × (1 + r/m)m×t, where P is principal, r is annual rate as a decimal, m is compounding periods per year and t is years. Interest earned is A − P. Actual institutions may use specific day counts, compounding conventions and rounding rules.

Cumulative versus periodic payout

A cumulative FD reinvests interest until maturity. A non-cumulative FD pays interest at selected intervals, such as monthly or quarterly, so the cash-flow pattern differs. Confirm which payout option the calculator models before comparing figures.

Example variables

Enter a hypothetical principal of ₹1,00,000, an assumed annual rate of 7% and a tenure of 2 years. Changing tenure or compounding frequency changes the projected value. The rate is an input assumption, not a live quote from a bank.

What to compare

  • Effective yield and interest payout frequency.
  • Premature withdrawal rules and penalties.
  • Auto-renewal and maturity payment instructions.
  • Tax treatment and any applicable TDS.
  • Institution status and applicable deposit-protection arrangements.

Tax and calculation limits

Interest may be taxable depending on current law and personal circumstances; TDS is not necessarily the final liability. Differences from a bank receipt may arise from actual deposit dates, leap years, day-count conventions, compounding and early closure. What to verify in a bank FD

Confirm the institution and product eligibility, minimum deposit, tenure range, compounding frequency, payout choice, senior-citizen rate eligibility, premature-withdrawal rules, auto-renewal instructions and maturity payment process. A cumulative deposit generally reinvests interest under its stated convention; a non-cumulative deposit pays interest at selected intervals, so the cash-flow pattern and maturity amount differ.

Tax, TDS and deposit protection

Interest taxation depends on current law, total income and personal circumstances. Tax deducted at source is a withholding mechanism and does not necessarily equal final tax liability. Check current thresholds and filing instructions with the Income Tax Department or a qualified tax professional. Deposit insurance and eligibility depend on the institution and account rules; consult the DICGC official website rather than assuming every deposit has identical coverage.

Common comparison mistakes

  • Comparing a cumulative maturity value with periodic-interest payouts without accounting for interim cash flows.
  • Using a promotional rate without checking the exact tenure band and eligibility.
  • Ignoring premature-closure penalties or special product conditions.
  • Treating a pre-tax maturity value as the amount that remains after tax.
  • Assuming the calculator's rounded estimate is the bank's contractual maturity quote.

Use this calculator for an initial scenario, then compare the bank's deposit advice and written terms. If the rate, compounding or dates differ, the bank's product terms govern the actual payout.

Guide to the FD Calculator: method, example and FAQs

How fixed deposit interest is computed

Banks in India typically compound FD interest quarterly, so the maturity value is A = P × (1 + r ÷ 400)4t with r as the annual percentage. With a cumulative deposit the interest is reinvested and paid at the end; with a payout option it is paid out monthly, quarterly or yearly and the principal stays constant.

Example: ₹1,00,000 at 7% for 3 years, compounded quarterly, matures at about ₹1,23,144, an interest of ₹23,144. The effective annual yield is 7.19%, slightly above the stated 7%.

Cumulative versus payout

Choose cumulative for a growing sum with a goal date, and payout when you need regular income, accepting that the payout version earns less in total because interest is not reinvested.

Check before booking

Interest is taxable as income, with TDS deducted above a limit; Form 15G or 15H can be given where eligible.

Premature withdrawal usually reduces the rate for the period run and may attract a penalty.

Senior citizens often get a higher rate; deposit insurance covers a limited sum per depositor per bank, so spread large deposits.
