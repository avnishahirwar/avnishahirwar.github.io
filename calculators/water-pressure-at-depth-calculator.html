How water pressure changes with depth

In a stationary fluid, hydrostatic gauge pressure increases with depth and fluid density. The calculator uses the entered density and depth to estimate this pressure difference.

Formula

Gauge pressure = density × gravity × depth (p = ρgh)

Worked example

For fresh water at a density of 1,000 kg/m³ and a depth of 10 m, the estimated gauge pressure is about 98.1 kPa using g = 9.807 m/s².

Assumptions and limitations

This is a simplified static-fluid estimate. Salinity, temperature, local gravity, waves, flow and the pressure reference affect real measurements. Absolute pressure also includes atmospheric pressure.
