Understanding BMI and its limitations

Body mass index is calculated from weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared. It is a screening measure at population level, not a direct measurement of body fat or an individual health diagnosis. It does not account for every factor that can affect health.

Use the result responsibly

Interpretation can differ for children and teenagers, pregnancy, athletes, older adults and some medical conditions. For anyone under 18, adult BMI categories should not be used; age- and sex-specific growth charts and a qualified healthcare professional are needed. Do not use this estimate as a target for restrictive eating or excessive exercise.

BMI calculator guide: meaning and limitations

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a screening measure calculated from weight and height. It is used in some population-level health assessments, but it does not directly measure body composition, fitness or overall health. A BMI result is not a diagnosis and should not be treated as a judgement about a person's appearance.

BMI formula

For metric inputs, BMI = weight in kilograms ÷ (height in metres × height in metres). For example, 70 kg and 1.75 m gives approximately 22.9. Unit conversion matters: height in centimetres must be converted to metres before applying the formula.

Important interpretation limits

  • BMI does not distinguish muscle from fat mass.
  • It does not show how body composition is distributed.
  • For children and teenagers, age- and sex-specific growth references are used rather than adult cut-offs.
  • Pregnancy, some medical conditions and individual differences can affect interpretation.
  • Health assessment may also consider medical history, blood pressure, nutrition, activity and other clinical factors.

Use the result carefully

A healthcare professional may use BMI as one screening indicator alongside other information. A single number cannot establish whether a person is healthy or unwell. If you have questions about growth, nutrition or health, discuss them with a qualified healthcare professional instead of making decisions from this calculator alone.

What the calculator does

The tool applies the height-and-weight formula to the numbers entered. It does not measure body composition, assess symptoms, diagnose disease or recommend a diet or exercise plan. Arithmetic accuracy depends on correct input values and units.

Privacy and reliable information

Height and weight can be personal health information. Enter only what is needed and avoid sharing sensitive details publicly. For interpretation, use recognised public-health guidance and a clinician who can consider age, development and medical history. BMI is one data point, not a complete description of a person.
