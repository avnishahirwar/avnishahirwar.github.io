How this 401(k) estimate works

The calculator applies your annual employee contribution and estimated employer match once per year, then applies the assumed annual return to the existing balance. Salary growth changes the contribution amount in later years. Contributions are modelled at year-end for a simple, consistent estimate.

Important assumptions

  • This is a simplified projection, not a forecast or investment recommendation.
  • It does not model taxes, fees, vesting schedules, contribution limits, catch-up rules, withdrawals, inflation or market volatility.
  • Employer matching rules differ by plan. Enter the matching percentage and salary cap from the plan documents.
  • Actual returns are not guaranteed and can be negative.

Review current plan terms and official IRS guidance before making decisions.

401(k) growth: example and how to read the result

Formula

Each year: employee contribution = salary × your contribution %; employer match = salary × min(your %, match cap %) × match %. The balance grows by the assumed annual return and contributions are added at year-end; salary then rises by the growth rate.

Worked example

Using the default inputs, a $10,000 starting balance, a $75,000 salary, 6% employee contribution, a 50% match on up to 6% of pay, 6% annual return and 2% salary growth over 20 years gives an estimated balance of about $322,522. Of that, your contributions total about $109,338 and the employer match about $54,669. This is an illustration, not a forecast.

Inputs explained

  • Employer match and cap: take these from your plan documents; a common design is 50% of the first 6% of pay.
  • Annual return: an assumption; real returns vary and can be negative.
  • Salary growth: raises the dollar contribution each year.

Common mistakes

  • Not contributing enough to receive the full employer match.
  • Using a high return assumption and treating the result as certain.
  • Forgetting that fees, taxes, vesting rules and IRS contribution limits are not modelled here.

Frequently asked questions

What is a 401(k) match?

Money your employer adds to your 401(k) based on your own contributions, up to a plan-defined limit.

Does this model contribution limits or taxes?

No. Check current IRS limits and your plan rules; this estimate omits taxes, fees, vesting and withdrawals.

Why does a small increase in contribution matter?

Contributions compound, so extra years and extra percentage points can change the final balance noticeably.

How to interpret this 401k result

This projection combines a starting 401(k) balance, employee contributions, an employer match, salary growth, and an assumed annual return. The match calculation depends on the plan’s matching percentage and the portion of pay eligible for that match, so use the plan document rather than assuming every employer contributes the same amount. Contributions are modelled at the timing described on the page; actual payroll deposits and market returns vary. The estimate does not include every plan fee, vesting rule, contribution limit, catch-up provision, loan, withdrawal, tax treatment, or investment allocation. A higher assumed return can materially change the result, but it is not a forecast or guarantee. Compare scenarios using the same time period and salary assumptions, then confirm contribution and match rules with the plan administrator.
