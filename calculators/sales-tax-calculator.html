Sales tax: formula, example and common mistakes

Formula

Sales tax = price × rate ÷ 100, and total = price + sales tax. To find the pre-tax price from a tax-inclusive total: price = total ÷ (1 + rate ÷ 100).

Worked example

An item priced at 250.00 with an 8.25% sales tax rate has tax of 20.63, so the total is 270.63. Working backwards, a total of 270.63 at the same rate corresponds to a pre-tax price of 250.00.

Inputs explained

  • Price: the pre-tax price of the item or service.
  • Tax rate (%): combine state, county and city rates if your location charges more than one; rates and taxable items vary by place.

Common mistakes

  • Using only the state rate when local rates also apply.
  • Assuming every item is taxable; groceries, medicine or services may be exempt in some places.
  • Applying the rate to a discounted price when the rules tax the original price.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate sales tax?

Multiply the price by the rate as a decimal, for example 0.0825 for 8.25%, and add the result to the price.

Is sales tax the same everywhere?

No. Rates, exemptions and rules differ by state, county and city, and by country.

Does this tool look up my local rate?

No. Enter the rate you want to use and confirm it with the relevant tax authority.
