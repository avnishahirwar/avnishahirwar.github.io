Formula and calculation method

Monthly rate r = annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100; n = years × 12. EMI = P × r × (1+r)^n ÷ ((1+r)^n − 1). At 0% interest, EMI = P ÷ n.

Worked example

For a ₹5,00,000 loan at 10% annually over 5 years, the monthly rate is 10% ÷ 12 and there are 60 instalments. The estimated EMI is ₹10,623.52, total repayment is ₹6,37,411.34 and total interest is ₹1,37,411.34. Values are rounded to two decimal places.

Important information

The result is an estimate, not a loan offer or approval. Lenders may apply processing fees, insurance, variable rates and different day-count conventions.

Loan EMI questions

How is monthly EMI calculated?

The calculator converts the annual interest rate into a monthly rate and applies the reducing-balance EMI formula across the total number of monthly instalments. At 0% interest, the principal is divided equally across the instalments.

Why does total interest increase when the tenure is longer?

A longer tenure leaves an outstanding loan balance for more months. The monthly EMI may fall, but the total interest paid can rise. Compare total repayment as well as the monthly amount.

Does the estimate include processing fees or insurance?

No. The calculation covers principal and scheduled interest only. Add lender fees, insurance, documentation charges and other costs separately when comparing a real offer.

Can the EMI change on a floating-rate loan?

Yes. A rate reset, revised benchmark or lender-specific repayment change can alter the EMI or remaining tenure. This illustration assumes one constant annual rate for the full term.

How to compare two loan offers

Keep the principal and tenure the same when comparing lenders, then change the annual interest rate. Review the monthly EMI alongside total repayment and total interest: a lower EMI can simply result from a longer tenure and may increase the overall borrowing cost.

Costs that may sit outside the EMI

Processing fees, documentation charges, insurance, floating-rate changes, late fees and prepayment conditions may not be included in this estimate. Ask the lender for the Key Facts Statement and amortisation schedule, and compare the annual percentage rate and total payable amount before accepting a loan. Loan EMI guide: principal, interest and repayment

An Equated Monthly Instalment (EMI) is a regular payment used to repay a loan over an agreed tenure. It usually contains both principal repayment and interest. This calculator estimates the instalment from the loan amount, annual interest rate and repayment period; it is useful for comparing scenarios before accepting an actual lender offer.

EMI formula explained

For a standard reducing-balance loan: EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1). Here P is principal, r is the monthly interest rate as a decimal, and n is the total number of monthly instalments. A nominal annual percentage rate is commonly converted as annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100. The formula assumes a constant rate and regular monthly payments.

Worked illustration

For a principal of ₹5,00,000 at a constant annual rate of 10% over 5 years, the monthly rate is 0.833333% and there are 60 instalments. The estimated EMI is ₹10,623.52, total scheduled repayment is ₹6,37,411.34 and total interest is ₹1,37,411.34. This is a reducing-balance illustration before fees, insurance or rate changes.

Compare loan offers fairly

  • Compare the same principal and tenure.
  • Check whether the rate is fixed, floating or reset periodically.
  • Review processing fees, insurance and documentation charges.
  • Compare total repayment, not EMI alone.
  • Read prepayment, foreclosure and late-payment conditions.

Why a lower EMI can cost more

A longer tenure generally lowers the monthly payment but can increase total interest because the balance remains outstanding longer. A shorter tenure may reduce interest but requires a higher monthly payment. Test realistic scenarios and leave room for essential expenses and unexpected costs.

Limitations

This estimate may not model moratoriums, step-up instalments, irregular disbursals, daily interest conventions or lender-specific charges. Verify the amortisation schedule and Key Facts Statement before accepting an offer.
