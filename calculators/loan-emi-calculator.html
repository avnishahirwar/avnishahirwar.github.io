Formula and calculation method

Monthly rate r = annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100; n = years × 12. EMI = P × r × (1+r)^n ÷ ((1+r)^n − 1). At 0% interest, EMI = P ÷ n.

Guide: understanding your Loan EMI Calculator results

How an EMI is calculated

An EMI is a fixed monthly payment that covers both interest and principal. Banks use the reducing-balance method: interest each month is charged only on the loan still outstanding. The standard formula is EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1), where P is the loan, r is the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of months. Early EMIs are mostly interest; later ones are mostly principal.

Why a longer tenure lowers the EMI but costs more

Stretching the tenure shrinks each instalment but gives interest more time to accumulate. For a ₹50,00,000 loan at 8.5%, a 20-year tenure has an EMI of ₹43,391 and total interest of ₹54,13,879. A 15-year tenure raises the EMI to ₹49,237 but cuts the interest to ₹38,62,656, a saving of ₹15,51,223 for about ₹5,846 more per month.

Using the result to check affordability

Lenders often look at the share of your monthly income that goes to EMIs. A common planning rule is to keep all EMIs within roughly 40% of take-home pay, but your own expenses matter more than any rule. Run the calculator at a rate 1–2 percentage points higher than your offer to see how a floating-rate increase would affect you.

Worked example with real numbers

Borrow ₹5,00,000 at 9% for 5 years. The EMI is ₹10,379.18, the total repayment is ₹6,22,750.66 and the interest is ₹1,22,750.66. If you prepay ₹1,00,000 after two years, the outstanding balance falls and you either shorten the tenure or lower the EMI, depending on what the lender allows, which reduces total interest.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Comparing loans by EMI alone instead of total interest and fees.
  • Ignoring processing fees, insurance and prepayment charges, which are not included in this estimate.
  • Entering the tenure in months in the years field.
  • Assuming a floating rate stays fixed for the whole tenure.

Important information

The result is an estimate, not a loan offer or approval. Lenders may apply processing fees, insurance, variable rates and different day-count conventions.

Frequently asked questions about the Loan EMI Calculator

Is the EMI the same on a flat-rate and a reducing-balance loan?

No. A flat-rate quote charges interest on the original amount for the whole tenure, so the effective rate is much higher than the headline figure. This calculator uses reducing balance, which is how home, car and most personal loans from banks are priced.

How much does a 0.5% rate change matter?

On ₹50,00,000 over 20 years, the difference between 8.5% and 9% adds roughly ₹2,000 to the monthly EMI and several lakh rupees to the total interest. Try both rates in the calculator to see the exact figure for your loan.

Can I use this for a car or education loan?

Yes, as long as the loan uses monthly reducing-balance repayment. Education loans may have a moratorium period during which interest accrues, which this basic tool does not model.

Why does my bank's EMI differ by a few rupees?

Banks round the monthly rate, the EMI and the first instalment date in their own way, and some count actual days in the first month. A difference of a few rupees is normal.

Does the EMI include GST or processing fee?

No. The tool shows only principal plus interest. Fees and GST on fees are charged separately by the lender.
