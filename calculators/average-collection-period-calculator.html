Average Collection Period Calculator

Calculate a period-level average using receivables and net credit sales from the same period. This ratio is not an invoice-aging report or a credit decision.

Calculator

Use credit sales only, net of applicable returns, allowances and discounts. Keep balances, sales and period length aligned.

Enter values and calculate.

Formula and method

Average accounts receivable = (beginning receivables + ending receivables) ÷ 2. Average collection period (days) = (average accounts receivable ÷ net credit sales for the period) × number of days in the period.

The calculation runs in your browser. Review the inputs, units and assumptions before relying on the result.

Worked example

With beginning receivables 8,000, ending receivables 12,000, net credit sales 100,000 and 365 days: average receivables = (8,000 + 12,000) ÷ 2 = 10,000; collection period = (10,000 ÷ 100,000) × 365 = 36.5 days.

Scope and limitations

  • The beginning/end average is a simplified estimate. Seasonal or volatile balances may require more frequent observations.
  • Use receivables and net credit sales from the same period; cash sales do not create receivables.
  • This is a high-level average, not a measure of whether a specific invoice is overdue or collectible.
  • Interpret the result with payment terms, prior periods and business context; it is not financial or credit advice.

Frequently asked questions

Should cash sales be included?

No. Cash sales do not create accounts receivable. Use net credit sales for the same period.

Does a shorter result always mean better performance?

Not necessarily. Compare the estimate with contractual terms, historical results, industry context and receivables aging.
