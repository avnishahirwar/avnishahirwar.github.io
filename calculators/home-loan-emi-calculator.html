EMI formula

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1), where P is principal, r is the monthly interest rate and n is the number of monthly instalments. At 0% interest, EMI = P ÷ n.

Frequently asked questions

How is home loan EMI calculated?

The calculator applies the reducing-balance EMI formula using a constant monthly rate and equal instalments.

Are processing fees and insurance included?

No. Fees, insurance, taxes and other lender charges are excluded.

Does this confirm home loan approval?

No. It is a mathematical estimate only. The lender determines eligibility and approval.

Who should use a home loan EMI calculator?

Home buyers comparing a loan amount, interest rate and repayment period can use this calculator to understand the monthly instalment and the total scheduled repayment under a fixed-rate model. It is useful for comparing scenarios before requesting a formal loan illustration from a lender.

Inputs explained

  • Loan amount: the principal expected to be borrowed, not necessarily the property's full purchase price. Account separately for down payment and transaction costs.
  • Annual interest rate: the annual rate used for this estimate. Enter the rate offered or assumed for comparison; the tool does not fetch live lender rates.
  • Tenure: the number of monthly instalments. A longer tenure can lower the EMI while increasing total interest.

Step-by-step EMI example

Suppose the loan principal is ₹30,00,000, the annual interest rate is 8.5% and the tenure is 240 months. The monthly rate is 8.5% ÷ 12 ÷ 100 and the reducing-balance formula estimates an EMI of about ₹26,034.70. Total scheduled repayment is about ₹62,48,327.28, including approximately ₹32,48,327.28 interest. Figures are rounded and exclude lender charges.

Change one input at a time to see how a different down payment, rate or tenure changes the estimate. Compare both EMI and total repayment.

Common mistakes and real-life use

  • Entering the property's full price instead of the amount financed after the down payment.
  • Comparing two offers with different tenures using EMI alone.
  • Ignoring processing fees, legal charges, insurance, taxes or rate-reset conditions.
  • Assuming the displayed rate will remain unchanged for a floating-rate loan.

A buyer can model a base offer and a shorter-tenure alternative, then ask the lender for a written amortisation schedule and a full list of charges.

Limitations

This calculator assumes a constant monthly interest rate and equal monthly instalments. It does not check income, credit history, property valuation, legal title, loan eligibility, floating-rate resets, prepayments, fees, insurance or lender-specific rounding. The result is an educational estimate, not an approval or offer.
