Age calculator guide: years, months and days

An age calculator finds the calendar difference between a date of birth and a selected end date. It can express the result in years, months and days, which is more informative than dividing elapsed days by 365. The result depends on the dates entered and the calendar rules used.

How calendar age is calculated

The calculation compares year, month and day components. If the end day is earlier than the birth day, the method borrows days from the previous calendar month; if the end month is earlier, it borrows a year. Month lengths differ and leap years add an extra day in February, so a fixed 365-day conversion is not sufficient for exact calendar age.

Choose and verify dates carefully

  • Enter the date of birth as recorded in the relevant document.
  • Choose the end date for which age is required.
  • Check day-month-year order to avoid reversing dates.
  • Consider the local date when a calculation is close to midnight.
  • For official applications, follow the authority's age cut-off rule.

Why official eligibility can differ

Some applications calculate age on a fixed cut-off date rather than the application date. Others specify that a person must have completed a certain age by a particular day. This tool shows a calendar difference but cannot interpret the eligibility wording of a recruitment notice, school admission rule, insurance contract or legal document.

Example

A person born on 15 June 2000 has not reached the 26th birthday on 10 June 2026. Read the result in completed years, months and days rather than rounding up to the next year. Verify the entered date against the source document.

Privacy and limitations

A date of birth is personal information. Enter only what is needed and avoid sharing identity documents or other sensitive details. This tool performs a date calculation; it does not verify identity or create an official age record. Use the relevant authority's instructions for legal or administrative eligibility decisions.
