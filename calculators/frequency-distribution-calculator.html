Formula and method

For each distinct value or class i, frequency fᵢ is its observation count. Total n = Σfᵢ. Relative frequency = fᵢ ÷ n; percentage = 100fᵢ ÷ n. For ordered numeric values, cumulative frequency is the running sum and cumulative relative frequency is cumulative frequency ÷ n.

The calculation runs in your browser. Review the inputs, units and assumptions before relying on the result.

Worked example

For the sample 20 student responses, values 2–7 have frequencies 3, 5, 3, 6, 2 and 1. The relative frequencies are 15%, 25%, 15%, 30%, 10% and 5%; the counts sum to 20 and percentages sum to 100%.

Scope and limitations

  • The table describes only the entered data and does not establish that the sample represents a broader population.
  • This version groups exact values or exact category labels; it does not create class intervals or grouped bins.
  • For text categories, cumulative statistics are omitted because nominal labels have no inherent order.
  • The dataset is limited to 1,000 observations and 10,000 characters; displayed percentages can show rounding.

Frequently asked questions

Can this create grouped class intervals?

No. It counts exact numeric values or text labels. Choose and validate bins separately if a grouped distribution is required.

Why are cumulative columns absent for text categories?

Cumulative counts need an order. Nominal labels such as colours have no natural ranking, so imposing one would be misleading.
