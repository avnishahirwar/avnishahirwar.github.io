Guide: understanding your EPF / PF Calculator results

How an EPF balance grows

Every month an employee's contribution (and the employer's share that goes to the EPF account) is added to the balance. EPFO declares an annual interest rate, and interest is calculated monthly on the running balance and credited at year-end. This calculator takes the total monthly amount actually credited to EPF and compounds it at the rate you enter.

What goes into the monthly figure

The employee contributes 12% of basic pay plus dearness allowance. The employer also contributes 12%, but part of it, 8.33% of wages up to the pension wage ceiling, goes to the pension scheme (EPS) rather than the EPF balance. If you enter ₹5,000, make sure it is the amount credited to the EPF account, not the whole 24%.

Using it for retirement planning

Over long periods, compounding does most of the work. A ₹5,000 monthly credit at 8.25% for 20 years grows to ₹30,38,301 on contributions of ₹12,00,000, so about ₹18,38,301 comes from interest. Raising the contribution when your pay rises has a bigger effect than small changes in the interest rate.

Worked example with real numbers

₹5,000 credited each month at 8.25% for 20 years gives an estimated balance of ₹30,38,301.09 on total contributions of ₹12,00,000, an estimated interest of ₹18,38,301.09. The real amount will differ because the EPF rate changes each year and your salary and contributions will change too.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Entering the full 24% instead of the amount credited to the EPF account.
  • Assuming today's interest rate is fixed for decades.
  • Not adjusting for salary growth, which raises later contributions.
  • Forgetting that withdrawing before five years of continuous service may attract tax.

Important information

This is not an EPFO passbook balance. Part of employer contribution may be allocated to EPS subject to applicable rules; interest rates can change.

Frequently asked questions about the EPF / PF Calculator

What interest rate should I enter?

Use the latest declared EPFO rate as a base case and also test a lower rate. Recent years have been in a range of roughly 8.1% to 8.25%, but the rate is set annually.

Is EPF interest taxable?

Interest on employee contributions above the annual threshold set in the rules can be taxable. Check the current limit with your payroll team.

Does this include VPF?

Yes, if you add your voluntary contribution into the monthly amount. VPF earns the same rate as EPF.

Why is my passbook balance different from the estimate?

The passbook credits interest annually after EPFO's declaration and uses the actual contribution history, which the estimate does not have.

Can I use this for PPF?

Not exactly. PPF has a different structure, with a yearly deposit limit and its own rate. A PPF calculator is planned.
