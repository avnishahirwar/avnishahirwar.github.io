What is basal metabolic rate (BMR)?

Basal metabolic rate, commonly shortened to BMR, is an estimate of the energy the body uses at rest to support essential functions. The Mifflin-St Jeor equation estimates BMR from weight, height, age and an equation variant. The result is expressed in kilocalories per day (kcal/day). It is a mathematical estimate, not a direct measurement of an individual's metabolism.

BMR is not the same as total daily energy expenditure. It does not include the energy used for walking, work, study, exercise, digestion or other daily activities. It should not be treated as a daily food target, a measure of health, a body-quality score or a recommendation to change eating habits.

Mifflin-St Jeor formula

For the equation variant coded as male: BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age (years) + 5.

For the equation variant coded as female: BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age (years) − 161.

The equation uses two constants from its original research model. These categories do not describe every person's biology or circumstances, and the equation may be less suitable for people whose characteristics differ from the population used to develop it. The output is rounded for display.

Worked example

For an adult aged 30, with a weight of 70 kg and height of 170 cm, using the equation variant with the +5 constant: 10 × 70 + 6.25 × 170 − 5 × 30 + 5 = 1,647.5 kcal/day. The displayed estimate rounds to about 1,648 kcal/day. This example explains the arithmetic only; it is not a suggested intake or target.

How to use this calculator

  1. Use this calculator only for an adult estimate. It is not intended for children or teenagers.
  2. Enter weight in kilograms and height in centimetres, using recent and reliable measurements if you already have them.
  3. Enter age in whole years and select the equation variant that matches the model you intend to examine.
  4. Select Calculate and read the result together with the limitations on this page. Do not use it to set a diet or restrict food.

What the result can and cannot tell you

The result can illustrate how the formula responds mathematically when one input changes. For example, changing the age or height input changes the equation output. It cannot identify a medical condition, evaluate nutrition status, diagnose a metabolic issue or determine an individual's exact energy needs. Real energy requirements are influenced by many factors that are not represented in this equation.

Because this page is intended for adult educational use, the age input is limited to 18 years and above. Children and teenagers have changing needs during growth and should not use an adult BMR estimate to guide eating or activity. If you have questions about nutrition, growth, illness, pregnancy, medication or changes in appetite, discuss them with a qualified healthcare professional.

Benefits and limitations

  • Shows the formula and units alongside the result.
  • Uses a commonly cited equation for an adult resting-energy estimate.
  • Lets an adult review the effect of changing a formula input.
  • Does not measure metabolism, activity level, body composition or health.
  • Does not provide a calorie target, weight-loss plan or dietary recommendation.

Common interpretation mistakes

Do not confuse BMR with total daily energy expenditure. Do not compare one person's result with another person's result as a judgement about health or appearance. Do not treat the equation as exact, and do not assume that a lower or higher number is inherently better. A calculator result is only as useful as the model's fit to the person and the quality of the inputs.

Frequently asked questions

What does BMR mean?

BMR is an estimate of energy used at rest for essential body functions. It is not a daily food target or a measure of health.

Which formula does this page use?

It uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, which estimates adult resting energy from weight, height, age and one of two equation constants.

Does BMR include exercise or daily activity?

No. The formula estimates resting energy only. It does not include movement, work, exercise or other daily activities.

Can teenagers use this estimate to decide how much to eat?

No. This adult calculator is not intended for children or teenagers. Growth and development affect energy needs; a qualified healthcare professional can provide age-appropriate guidance.

Is this result a medical measurement?

No. It is a formula-based estimate and cannot diagnose a condition or measure an individual's metabolism. Discuss health questions with a qualified healthcare professional.
