Formula and calculation method

Simple estimate = balance × annual rate ÷ 100 × days ÷ 365. Leap-year and bank-specific rules may vary.

Worked example

For ₹50,000 at 3% annually over 90 days, estimate = ₹50,000 × 0.03 × 90 ÷ 365.

Guide: understanding your Savings Account Interest Calculator results

How savings interest is estimated

Banks calculate interest on the daily closing balance and credit it quarterly or monthly, depending on the bank. This tool uses the average balance, the annual rate and the number of days: interest = balance × rate ÷ 100 × days ÷ 365. With ₹50,000 at 3% for 365 days, the estimate is ₹1,500.

Why the average balance matters

If your balance moves up and down, take the average of the daily closing balances over the period. A balance of ₹1,00,000 for 15 days and ₹10,000 for 15 days averages ₹55,000, which is what you should enter for 30 days.

Tax on savings interest

Interest from a savings account is taxable under 'income from other sources'. Under the old regime, a deduction is available for savings interest up to a limit (higher for senior citizens). Check which regime you use before assuming a deduction.

Worked example with real numbers

₹50,000 average balance at 3% for 365 days: estimated interest ₹1,500, balance with interest ₹51,500. Moving the same amount to a deposit at 7% would earn about ₹3,500, so keeping idle cash above what you need for emergencies in a savings account costs about ₹2,000 a year.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using the opening balance instead of the average balance.
  • Ignoring that the credit date is quarterly, so the interest is not visible until then.
  • Forgetting the minimum balance charges of some accounts.
  • Treating the estimate as a bank statement figure.

Important information

Banks may credit interest quarterly or at another interval. Tax treatment is not included; check account terms and current tax rules.

Frequently asked questions about the Savings Account Interest Calculator

Why is my actual interest different?

Banks apply their own rounding and slab rates (a higher rate on higher balances in some accounts) and credit at fixed dates.

What is a sweep-in account?

A savings account linked to an FD. Balance above a set limit is moved to a deposit automatically and earns the FD rate.

How many days should I enter for a quarter?

Enter 90 to 92 days depending on the quarter, or the exact count between the credit dates.

Is the 365-day year used by every bank?

Most Indian banks use 365 days in the year. Leap years may be treated differently by some banks.

Is there TDS on savings interest?

A bank deducts TDS only if interest crosses the annual threshold under the tax rules. Check the current limit.
