Formula and calculation method

Maximum EMI capacity = monthly income × FOIR limit − existing monthly obligations. The indicative loan amount is the present value of that EMI stream at the assumed monthly rate and tenure.

Understanding the result

Compare both the periodic payment and the total amount paid. A longer tenure can reduce the monthly payment while increasing total interest. Fees, insurance, taxes, rate resets, repayment dates and provider-specific terms may affect the actual amount.

Who should use this estimate?

This calculator is for someone who wants to test how existing monthly obligations and an assumed fixed-obligation-to-income ratio (FOIR) affect a possible EMI budget. It is a planning illustration only. A lender's eligibility assessment can use different income definitions, obligations, credit information, policies and verification.

Inputs explained

  • Monthly take-home income: the monthly income figure used by this model. Lenders may use verified net or gross income and may treat variable pay differently.
  • Existing monthly obligations: current EMIs and other obligations included in your own affordability assumption.
  • Maximum EMI share (FOIR): the share of monthly income assumed to be available for total obligations. This is user-selected, not a universal lender rule.
  • Annual interest rate and tenure: assumptions used to convert the remaining EMI capacity into an indicative principal amount.

Worked example

Assume monthly take-home income of ₹60,000, existing obligations of ₹10,000 and a selected FOIR of 40%. The model's maximum EMI capacity is ₹60,000 × 40% − ₹10,000 = ₹14,000. At an assumed 12% annual rate over five years, that EMI corresponds to an indicative loan amount of about ₹6,29,371 under the reducing-balance formula. This is not an offer or approval amount.

Common mistakes and practical use

  • Using a FOIR percentage as if every bank applies the same threshold.
  • Leaving out existing EMIs, card obligations or other recurring commitments.
  • Using income before deductions when the selected model expects take-home income.
  • Choosing a long tenure only to make the estimated amount appear larger without checking total interest.

Use the result to prepare questions for a lender and compare affordability scenarios. Keep a separate household budget for essential expenses and irregular costs; the calculator does not know your full financial circumstances.
