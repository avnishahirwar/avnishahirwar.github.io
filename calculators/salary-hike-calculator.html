Guide to the Salary Hike Calculator: method, example and FAQs

Working out a hike properly

A salary hike is the percentage change between your old and new pay. The formula is Hike % = (New − Old) ÷ Old × 100, and the reverse, when an employer offers a percentage, is New = Old × (1 + Hike ÷ 100). Always compare like with like: either the annual CTC on both sides, or the monthly in-hand pay on both sides.

Example: moving from ₹6,00,000 to ₹6,90,000 a year is a hike of ₹90,000, which is 15.0% of the old figure. Monthly, that is ₹50,000 rising to ₹57,500 in CTC terms.

Adjust for inflation to see the real raise

If prices rise 6% in the same year, a 15% hike is not a 15% improvement in what you can buy. The real hike is (1.15 ÷ 1.06) − 1 = 8.49%. A hike lower than inflation is a pay cut in real terms even though the number on the payslip goes up.

Why in-hand pay rises by less than CTC

CTC includes items that are not paid monthly, such as employer provident fund contribution, gratuity, insurance and any variable pay. When CTC rises, the tax slab effect and statutory deductions mean take-home usually rises by a smaller percentage. For a clean comparison, ask for the new salary structure and compare the fixed monthly credit.

Using the number in a negotiation

Switching employers commonly involves asking for a larger percentage than an internal appraisal. Work out the figure that keeps your real pay growing, add the value of benefits that are changing, and keep the final CTC figure in writing before you resign.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate salary hike percentage?

(New salary − old salary) ÷ old salary × 100.

What is the hike from ₹6,00,000 to ₹6,90,000?

15.0%.

Should I compare CTC or in-hand salary?

Compare the same measure on both sides. If you can, compare both, since the gap between them changes with the structure.

What is a good hike?

One that beats inflation and reflects your market value; there is no universal figure, so check the role’s market pay.
