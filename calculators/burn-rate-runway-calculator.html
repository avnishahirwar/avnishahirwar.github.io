Worked burn-rate and runway example

Assume available cash is ₹10,00,000, monthly operating costs are ₹2,00,000 and monthly operating revenue is ₹1,00,000. Net monthly cash burn is ₹2,00,000 − ₹1,00,000 = ₹1,00,000. Estimated runway is ₹10,00,000 ÷ ₹1,00,000 = 10 months, assuming cash, costs and revenue remain unchanged. This is a simple cash-planning estimate, not a forecast: one-off payments, collections timing, taxes, financing and changes in sales or costs can materially alter runway.

Burn-rate questions

What is net burn rate?

Net burn is monthly operating costs minus monthly operating revenue. If revenue exceeds costs, the tool shows an operating surplus rather than a positive cash burn.

How is runway estimated?

When monthly net burn is positive, runway is available cash divided by monthly net burn. The estimate assumes a constant burn rate and does not model future changes month by month.

Should I include one-time expenses?

For a short-term cash view, include expected cash expenses in the period you are analysing. For a recurring operating burn estimate, separate unusual one-time items so they do not distort the recurring monthly figure.

Why can actual runway differ from this result?

Customer payment delays, seasonality, payroll changes, taxes, debt repayments, capital expenditure and unexpected costs can change cash balances. Recalculate with updated figures and a realistic cash-flow forecast.
