Estimate an electricity bill from units

Electricity consumption is commonly shown in kilowatt-hours (kWh), often called units on a household bill. A simple estimate multiplies units by the selected per-unit rate and then adds fixed or other charges entered. Actual bills may use different slabs for different portions of consumption.

Why the bill may differ

State distribution-company tariffs can include slab rates, fixed charges, fuel adjustment, electricity duty, meter rent, subsidies and time-of-use rules. Enter the charges that apply to your connection and check the latest tariff order or bill from your distribution company. This is a planning estimate, not a bill issued by the utility.

Electricity bill calculator guide: estimate energy charges

An electricity bill calculator estimates energy cost from units consumed and a tariff assumption. One unit generally represents one kilowatt-hour (kWh). Actual bills may include slab rates, fixed charges, fuel adjustments, duties, taxes, meter rent and other items that vary by distribution company and consumer category.

Basic electricity consumption formula

Energy used in kWh = Power in kilowatts × Hours of use. A 1 kW appliance used for 3 hours consumes 3 kWh. For a 100-watt appliance, convert watts to kilowatts by dividing by 1,000: 0.1 kW × 5 hours = 0.5 kWh. Multiply units by the applicable rate only when a single-rate estimate is suitable.

Estimate household usage

List each appliance, rated wattage, average daily hours and number of days. Multiply kW by hours and days to estimate monthly units, then add the appliances. Real consumption depends on thermostat settings, usage patterns, appliance condition, standby power and voltage. Nameplate wattage is a guide, not necessarily a constant draw.

Understand the tariff

  • Domestic tariffs may use consumption slabs.
  • Fixed or customer charges may apply even at low usage.
  • Fuel or power-purchase adjustments may appear.
  • Duties, taxes and subsidies may depend on state and category.
  • Some connections have time-of-day or demand charges.

Check the estimate against your bill

Use the meter-reading difference for the billing period where available. Confirm consumer category, sanctioned load and current tariff schedule from the distribution company. Compare energy charges separately from fixed charges and other bill components.

Limitations

This calculator is not connected to a smart meter or utility billing system. It cannot confirm your tariff, subsidy eligibility or final bill. Use the utility bill and current tariff order for payment decisions. For wiring or equipment issues, consult a qualified electrician rather than attempting unsafe electrical changes.
