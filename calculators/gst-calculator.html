Formula and calculation method

GST on base amount = amount × rate ÷ 100. Total = amount + GST. For an inclusive price, taxable value = inclusive amount ÷ (1 + rate ÷ 100); GST = inclusive amount − taxable value.

Worked example

A taxable value of ₹10,000 at 18% gives GST of ₹1,800 and a total of ₹11,800. If ₹11,800 already includes 18% GST, the embedded GST is ₹1,800.

Guide: understanding your GST Calculator results

How GST is added to a price

GST is charged on the taxable value of a supply. If you sell something for ₹25,000 before tax and the rate is 18%, the tax is ₹25,000 × 18 ÷ 100 = ₹4,500 and the invoice total is ₹29,500. For a sale within one state, that ₹4,500 is split equally into CGST and SGST (₹2,250 each). For a sale to another state it is charged as IGST (₹4,500). The total to the customer is the same either way; only the heads under which it is reported differ.

How to take GST out of a GST-inclusive price

Retail prices in India are usually printed with tax already included. To find the base price you divide by (1 + rate), not subtract the rate. A ₹5,900 inclusive price at 18% has a base of ₹5,900 ÷ 1.18 = ₹5,000 and GST of ₹900. Subtracting 18% of ₹5,900 (₹1,062) would give the wrong answer, which is the most common GST mistake on small-business invoices.

Which rate applies

Rates depend on the HSN code (goods) or SAC code (services), not on how the product is described. This tool lets you pick 0.25%, 3%, 5%, 18% or 40%, but it cannot decide the classification for you. Check the notification or ask your CA before invoicing; the wrong rate can lead to short payment of tax, interest and notices.

Worked example with real numbers

A shop buys stock for ₹25,000 plus 18% GST. The calculator shows GST of ₹4,500 and a total of ₹29,500. The shop can claim the ₹4,500 as input tax credit if it is a registered taxpayer and the other conditions are met. When it sells the stock for ₹32,000 plus 18% GST it collects ₹5,760, pays only the difference (₹5,760 − ₹4,500 = ₹1,260) to the government, and keeps the credit trail for its returns.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Applying the rate to an inclusive price instead of dividing by (1 + rate).
  • Rounding each line separately and then finding the invoice total does not match the sum; round at the level your invoice format requires.
  • Using one rate for a bundled supply when the items have different rates.
  • Forgetting that registration is required above the turnover threshold for your state and type of supply, so check the current limit.

Important information

GST is generally split into CGST and SGST for intra-state supplies, while IGST generally applies to inter-state supplies. This tool shows combined GST. Select a rate only after checking official classification guidance.

Frequently asked questions about the GST Calculator

Is GST on a ₹1,000 item with 18% GST ₹180 or ₹152.54?

Both can be right depending on what ₹1,000 means. If ₹1,000 is the price before tax, GST is ₹180 and you pay ₹1,180. If ₹1,000 is the final price including tax, the GST inside it is ₹152.54 and the base price is ₹847.46.

What is the difference between CGST, SGST and IGST?

CGST and SGST are charged together on supplies within one state and go to the Centre and the state. IGST is charged on supplies between states and on imports. The total rate is the same; this calculator shows the combined GST.

Can I use this to prepare a tax invoice?

You can use it to check the numbers, but a valid tax invoice must contain the supplier's GSTIN, HSN/SAC, place of supply and other fields required by law. Use proper billing software for the invoice itself.

Why is the 0% or 12% option not offered?

The tool follows the structure in force after the September 2025 rationalisation, where most items are at 5% or 18% with 40% for a small set of items. Exempt or nil-rated supplies have no tax to calculate.

Does the result change if I enter a decimal amount?

No. The calculator works with decimals and shows the result to two places. Your accounts may round differently, so match your invoicing software if the paise matter.
