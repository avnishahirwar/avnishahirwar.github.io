Formula and methodology

This calculator uses a conventional calendar estimate. It counts the number of days from the first day of the last menstrual period (LMP) to the calculation date. It estimates the due date as 280 days, or 40 weeks, after the LMP date. The result is expressed as completed weeks plus remaining days.

The estimate is based on menstrual dating, not a scan or clinical examination. It uses a conventional 28-day cycle assumption and does not adjust the date for a longer or shorter cycle, uncertain dates, irregular periods, fertility treatment or other individual circumstances. The trimester labels in this tool use approximate boundaries at week 14 and week 28. Different clinical resources may describe boundary weeks slightly differently.

For example, if the first day of the last menstrual period was 1 March, adding 280 calendar days gives an estimated due date in early December of the same year. The exact date depends on the calendar year and leap-year rules; the calculator performs the date arithmetic directly rather than assuming every month has 30 days.

How to use this pregnancy calculator

  1. Select the first day of your last menstrual period. Use the date you recorded, not an estimated conception date.
  2. Select the date for which you want to calculate gestational age. It may be today or another date on or after the LMP date.
  3. Choose Calculate and review the estimated weeks, days, trimester and due date shown in the result area.
  4. Discuss the estimate with a qualified healthcare professional, especially if your cycle is irregular, the LMP date is uncertain or a scan gives a different date.

What the result means

Gestational age is commonly counted from the first day of the last menstrual period, which is usually earlier than the date of conception. This is why a pregnancy dated at four weeks does not mean conception occurred four weeks earlier. The LMP convention provides a consistent starting point for an initial estimate, but it cannot establish the exact day of ovulation or conception.

The estimated due date is a planning reference, not a deadline or a prediction of the exact day a baby will be born. Pregnancy length and individual circumstances vary. A healthcare professional may review menstrual history, ultrasound measurements and other clinical information to confirm or revise dating.

Benefits and limitations

  • Converts calendar dates into completed weeks and remaining days.
  • Shows an estimated due date using the standard 280-day convention.
  • Provides a simple trimester estimate based on the boundaries stated on this page.
  • Runs in the browser using the two dates entered; it does not require names or account details.
  • Does not diagnose pregnancy, assess fetal development, determine health status or replace prenatal care.

Use a date from a reliable personal record when possible. If you are unsure about the date, do not treat a calculator result as confirmation. A clinician can help interpret the dates and decide whether further assessment is appropriate.

Important information for different cycle lengths

The simple LMP method is easiest to interpret when cycles are reasonably regular and close to 28 days. If cycles are usually longer or shorter, ovulation may not occur around the same cycle day assumed by the conventional method. This calculator has no cycle-length adjustment field, so it cannot personalise the due-date estimate for that difference.

People who do not know their LMP date, have irregular cycles, recently stopped hormonal contraception, are breastfeeding, or conceived with fertility treatment may need a different dating approach. Do not try to correct the result by guessing. Share the relevant history with a qualified healthcare professional.

Frequently asked questions

Is this calculator a medical diagnosis?

No. It performs calendar arithmetic only. It cannot confirm pregnancy or assess maternal or fetal health. A qualified healthcare professional can provide clinical assessment and advice.

Why is pregnancy counted from the last menstrual period?

LMP is a commonly used reference date for an initial gestational-age estimate. It is generally earlier than conception, so gestational age and time since conception are not the same.

Does this calculator account for my cycle length?

No. It uses the conventional 280-day estimate and does not adjust for a cycle shorter or longer than 28 days. Ask a healthcare professional to interpret dates when cycles are irregular or the LMP date is uncertain.

Can an ultrasound give a different due date?

Yes. A clinician may use ultrasound findings and your medical history to assess pregnancy dating. Discuss differences with your healthcare professional rather than choosing one date yourself.

Does the due date tell me the exact day of birth?

No. It is an estimate for planning and follow-up, not a prediction of the exact birth date.
