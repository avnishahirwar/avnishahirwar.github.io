Worked examples

Use these examples to check the calculator and understand the result before using it for your own figures:

  • Addition: 125 + 75 = 200. This combines two amounts into one total.
  • Subtraction: 500 − 135 = 365. This finds the difference between two values.
  • Multiplication: 24 × 6 = 144. This is useful for repeated quantities, such as the cost of six items priced at 24 each.
  • Division: 960 ÷ 8 = 120. This splits a total into eight equal parts. Division by zero is undefined.
  • Percentage: 15% of 800 = 120. Convert the percentage to a decimal (15 ÷ 100 = 0.15), then multiply 800 × 0.15.
  • Decimals: 12.5 + 2.75 = 15.25. Enter the decimal point using the keypad or keyboard.

Tips for reliable results

  • Check the digits and decimal point before pressing equals.

  • For a percentage of an amount, divide the percentage by 100 and multiply by the amount.
  • Use Backspace to correct the current entry or AC to clear the calculator and begin again.
  • For important financial or business calculations, independently verify the inputs and final result.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use this basic calculator on a phone?

Yes. The keypad is designed for touch input on phones and tablets, and the supported keyboard shortcuts can be used on compatible physical keyboards.

Does the calculator support decimal numbers?

Yes. Enter a decimal point as part of a number, such as 12.5 or 0.75. The displayed result is formatted for practical everyday use.

How do I calculate a percentage?

To find 15% of 800, calculate 800 × 15 ÷ 100. The result is 120. For a different percentage, replace 15 and 800 with your own values.

Why should I verify an important result?

Displayed results depend on the values entered and ordinary JavaScript number arithmetic. For tax, loan, accounting or other consequential decisions, check the assumptions and verify the calculation against an authoritative source.

Basic calculator guide: everyday arithmetic

A basic calculator performs common operations such as addition, subtraction, multiplication and division. It is useful for checking totals, comparing quantities and calculating simple percentages. It is intended for straightforward arithmetic rather than advanced scientific, financial or statistical analysis.

Four basic operations

  • Addition: combine quantities, such as 125 + 75 = 200.
  • Subtraction: find a difference, such as 200 − 75 = 125.
  • Multiplication: combine equal groups, such as 12 × 8 = 96.
  • Division: split into equal parts, such as 96 ÷ 8 = 12.

Percentages in everyday use

To find x% of a number, multiply by x and divide by 100. For example, 15% of 800 is 120. To apply a percentage increase, multiply by 1 + x/100; for a decrease, multiply by 1 − x/100. These operations are useful for estimates but do not determine tax or legal treatment.

Order of operations

When an expression contains multiple operations, follow the standard order and use parentheses to make the intended sequence clear. Multiplication and division are evaluated before addition and subtraction, with equal-precedence operations handled from left to right. For example, 2 + 3 × 4 = 14, while (2 + 3) × 4 = 20.

Decimals and rounding

Keep enough decimal places during intermediate steps, especially for money, measurements and percentages. Rounding too early can create small differences. For financial records, follow the rounding convention required by the relevant invoice, contract or accounting system.

Input tips and limitations

Check the displayed operator before calculating and use clear or backspace controls to correct an entry. For a long expression, break it into smaller steps or use parentheses if supported. A basic calculator does not provide advanced scientific functions unless they are shown on the page, and it cannot validate the meaning of an input or replace documented professional calculations.
