Formula and calculation method

Profit/loss = selling price − cost price. Percentage = absolute difference ÷ cost price × 100. Positive means profit; negative means loss.

Worked example

A total cost of ₹800 and selling price of ₹1,000 gives ₹200 profit and 25% profit on cost. This differs from margin on sales.

Important information

Profit percentage on cost is not the same as gross margin. Gross margin is generally profit divided by selling price.

Profit and loss: a worked business example

If an item costs ₹800 and sells for ₹1,000, the gross difference is ₹200. Profit percentage on cost is ₹200 ÷ ₹800 × 100 = 25%. The margin on selling price is a different measure: ₹200 ÷ ₹1,000 × 100 = 20%. Always state which percentage you are using.

Costs that may be missing

A simple cost-price and selling-price comparison may exclude freight, packaging, platform commissions, payment fees, returns, discounts, overhead, GST treatment and income tax. Include relevant business costs before treating the result as net profit. This calculator is a basic trading estimate, not a substitute for bookkeeping or tax advice.
