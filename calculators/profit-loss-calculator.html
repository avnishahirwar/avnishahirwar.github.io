Formula and calculation method

Profit/loss = selling price − cost price. Percentage = absolute difference ÷ cost price × 100. Positive means profit; negative means loss.

Worked example

A total cost of ₹800 and selling price of ₹1,000 gives ₹200 profit and 25% profit on cost. This differs from margin on sales.

Guide: understanding your Profit &amp; Loss Calculator results

Profit, loss and percentage on cost

Profit is selling price minus cost price; if it is negative, it is a loss. The percentage is calculated on the cost price: profit % = (SP − CP) ÷ CP × 100. Buying at ₹800 and selling at ₹1,000 gives a profit of ₹200, which is 25% on cost. Selling the same item at ₹700 is a loss of ₹100, or 12.5% of cost.

Markup versus margin

These are often confused. Markup is profit as a share of cost; margin is profit as a share of selling price. The ₹200 profit above is a 25% markup but only a 20% margin (200 ÷ 1,000). If your target is a 20% margin, a 20% markup on cost would give just a 16.7% margin, so the difference changes your prices.

Beyond the basic calculation

This tool uses cost and selling price only. A real business also pays for rent, wages, packaging, delivery, returns, platform fees and GST. Include these in the cost price to see the profit that reaches you.

Worked example with real numbers

Cost ₹800, selling ₹1,000: profit ₹200, 25.00% on cost, 20% margin on selling price. Cost ₹800, selling ₹700: loss ₹100, 12.50% on cost. To reach a 30% margin on a cost of ₹800, divide by 0.70: the selling price must be about ₹1,142.86.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Calculating profit percentage on the selling price and calling it markup.
  • Ignoring GST, shipping and returns in the cost.
  • Setting prices from the competitor without checking your own cost.
  • Forgetting that discounts reduce the selling price and therefore the margin.

Important information

Profit percentage on cost is not the same as gross margin. Gross margin is generally profit divided by selling price.

Frequently asked questions about the Profit &amp; Loss Calculator

How do I find the selling price for a target profit?

For a target markup on cost, selling price = cost × (1 + markup %). For a target margin on selling price, selling price = cost ÷ (1 − margin %).

What is the break-even selling price?

The cost price including all expenses. Selling above it gives profit, below it gives a loss.

How do discounts affect profit?

A 10% discount on a product with a 20% margin can reduce profit by half or more, because the discount comes out of the margin.

Is GST part of the profit?

No. GST collected is paid to the government. Calculate profit on prices excluding GST.

Can it handle multiple items?

It calculates one item at a time. Add up the costs and sales to find totals for a batch.
