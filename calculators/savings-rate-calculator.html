Savings Rate Calculator

Find the share of your income you save each month and how much that adds up to over a year.

Formula and methodology

Savings rate (%) = (income − spending) ÷ income × 100. Annual savings = monthly savings × 12.

Worked example

With Monthly take-home income 80,000, Monthly spending 52,000, the calculator gives: Monthly savings = 28,000; Savings rate (%) = 35; Annual savings = 3,36,000.

How to read the result

Savings rate is a stronger planning signal than any single investment return because it is fully in your control. Use the same definition of income each month so trends are comparable.

Frequently asked questions

What if my spending is higher than income?

The calculator shows zero savings. Review recurring costs or raise income before planning investments.

Should I use gross or take-home income?

Take-home income is simpler. If you use gross income, state that so the rate is not compared against a take-home figure.

How to interpret this savings rate result

Savings rate measures the share of take-home income left after the monthly spending amount entered. It is a budgeting indicator, not an investment return: the result describes how much income is retained, not how quickly that money grows. Use a consistent definition of income and spending each month, and include irregular costs such as insurance, annual fees, repairs, and medical or education expenses when comparing periods. If you use gross income instead of take-home pay, state that clearly because the percentage will not be directly comparable with a net-income calculation. The calculator does not classify expenses, assess debt affordability, or account for household members’ shared finances. Review the trend over several months rather than relying on a single unusually high or low month.
