Formula and calculation method

Discount = original price × discount percentage ÷ 100. Sale price = original price − discount.

Worked example

A ₹2,000 product with a 15% discount has a ₹300 discount and a sale price of ₹1,700 before additional charges.

Guide: understanding your Discount Calculator results

Finding the sale price and the saving

A discount percentage is applied to the original price. A ₹4,999 item at 30% off saves ₹1,499.70, so you pay ₹3,499.30. To check a deal quickly, multiply the original price by (1 − discount ÷ 100); here, 4,999 × 0.70 = 3,499.30.

Why two discounts do not add up

Successive discounts apply to the already reduced price. Take 30% off and then a further 10% off ₹4,999: after the first discount the price is ₹3,499.30; the second takes off ₹349.93 and leaves ₹3,149.37. The total saving is ₹1,849.63, an effective 37%, not 40%. When a store advertises 'extra 10% off' on top of a sale, calculate the combined effect in two steps.

Discounts and GST

Whether GST is charged on the discounted or the original price depends on how the discount is given. A discount shown on the invoice and known at the time of supply generally reduces the taxable value. Always compare the final payable price, including any delivery or convenience fee, across sellers.

Worked example with real numbers

A jacket is listed at ₹4,999 with 30% off. The calculator shows a saving of ₹1,499.70 and a final price of ₹3,499.30. If a coupon takes a further 10% off, you pay ₹3,149.37, an overall discount of about 37%.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Adding two percentage discounts together.
  • Comparing 'up to 70% off' labels instead of the actual price of the item you want.
  • Forgetting delivery charges or the loss of cashback when comparing offers.
  • Calculating the discount on the sale price when the percentage refers to the maximum retail price (MRP).

Important information

The calculator does not decide tax treatment. Check whether the seller applies the discount before or after tax on the invoice.

Frequently asked questions about the Discount Calculator

How do I find the original price from the sale price?

Divide the sale price by (1 − discount ÷ 100). If you paid ₹3,499.30 after 30% off, the original was 3,499.30 ÷ 0.70 = ₹4,999.

Is a 50% discount the same as buy one get one free?

Per item, yes: you pay half the price either way when you buy two. The difference is that BOGO forces you to buy two, so it is only a saving if you would have bought both.

How do I compare a percentage discount with a flat discount?

Convert the flat amount to a percentage by dividing it by the price. ₹500 off a ₹2,000 item is 25%, but ₹500 off a ₹10,000 item is only 5%.

Does the calculator handle the MRP rule?

It does not know the MRP. Enter the price the discount actually applies to, whether that is the MRP or a listed price.

Can I use it for markups?

A markup is the reverse operation. For that, use the profit and loss calculator, which works from the cost price.
