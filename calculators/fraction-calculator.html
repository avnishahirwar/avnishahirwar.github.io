How fraction calculations work

A fraction represents a numerator divided by a non-zero denominator. For addition and subtraction, both fractions are converted to a common denominator. For multiplication, multiply the numerators and denominators. For division, multiply the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

Add / subtract: a/b ± c/d = (ad ± bc) / bd
Multiply: a/b × c/d = ac / bd
Divide: a/b ÷ c/d = ad / bc, where c is not zero.

The final numerator and denominator are divided by their greatest common divisor (GCD). The denominator is displayed as positive.

Worked example: 3/4 + 2/5

The common denominator of 4 and 5 is 20. Convert 3/4 to 15/20 and 2/5 to 8/20. Add the numerators: 15/20 + 8/20 = 23/20. This is already in simplest form and can also be written as 1 3/20 or 1.15.

What is a simplified fraction?

A fraction is in simplest form when its numerator and denominator have no common positive factor other than 1. For example, 6/8 reduces to 3/4 by dividing both values by 2.

Frequently asked questions

Can the calculator handle negative fractions?

Yes. Enter a negative numerator to represent a negative fraction. The result is normalised with a positive denominator.

Why can the denominator not be zero?

Division by zero is undefined, so a fraction with a zero denominator is not valid.

How do I convert a decimal to a fraction?

Write the decimal digits over a power of ten, then reduce by the greatest common divisor. For example, 0.625 = 625/1000 = 5/8. This calculator focuses on operations between two fractions.

What happens when I divide by zero?

The calculator displays a validation message because division by zero has no defined result.
