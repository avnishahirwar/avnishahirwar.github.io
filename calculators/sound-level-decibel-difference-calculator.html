Formula and methodology

Sound level L = 10 × log₁₀(I ÷ I₀) with I₀ = 10⁻¹² W/m². Difference = 10 × log₁₀(I₂ ÷ I₁).

Worked example

With Intensity 1 0.000001, Intensity 2 0.0001, the calculator gives: Level of sound 1 (dB) = 60; Level of sound 2 (dB) = 80; Difference (dB) = 20.

How to read the result

Every 10 dB step is a tenfold change in intensity, although perceived loudness grows more slowly. Long exposure to high levels can damage hearing.

Frequently asked questions

Why use a logarithmic scale?

The ear responds to a huge range of intensities, so a log scale keeps the numbers manageable.

Is the difference the same as adding levels?

No. Combining two sources requires adding intensities first, not decibel values.

Assumptions and limitations

This tool uses idealised textbook formulas for learning and quick estimates. Real systems include friction, losses, tolerances and measurement error. Do not use it as the only basis for safety-critical or regulated engineering design.
