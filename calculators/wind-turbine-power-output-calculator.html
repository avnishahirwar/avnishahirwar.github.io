Wind Turbine Power Output Calculator

Estimate the power available from wind using rotor diameter, wind speed, air density and an efficiency coefficient.

Enter your values

Formula and methodology

Power = ½ × air density × swept area × wind speed³ × power coefficient. The theoretical maximum coefficient is about 0.593 (Betz limit).

Worked example

With Rotor diameter 10, Wind speed 8, Air density 1.225, Power coefficient 0.35, the calculator gives: Swept area (m²) = 78.54; Wind power in the swept area (W) = 24,630.09; Estimated electrical output (W) = 8,620.53; Estimated electrical output (kW) = 8.62.

How to read the result

Power rises with the cube of wind speed, so a site with slightly stronger wind produces far more energy. Real output also depends on cut-in speed, gearbox and generator losses.

Frequently asked questions

What is a realistic power coefficient?

Small modern turbines often reach about 0.30 to 0.45 in good conditions.

Is this annual energy production?

No. It is instantaneous power at one wind speed, not yearly output.

Assumptions and limitations

This tool uses idealised textbook formulas for learning and quick estimates. Real systems include friction, losses, tolerances and measurement error. Do not use it as the only basis for safety-critical or regulated engineering design.
