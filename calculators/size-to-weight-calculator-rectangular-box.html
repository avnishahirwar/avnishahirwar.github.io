Size to Weight Calculator (Rectangular Box)

Estimate the mass of a solid rectangular block from its dimensions and material density.

Enter your values

Formula

Rectangular volume = length × width × height. Estimated mass = volume × material density. Equivalent weight force = mass × 9.80665 m/s².

Dimensions are converted to metres before volume is calculated. Density must be entered in kg/m³.

How to use this calculator

  1. Enter the three dimensions and choose the same unit for all of them.
  2. Enter the material density from a reliable specification or measurement.
  3. Select Calculate to see estimated mass and the corresponding force under standard gravity.

Worked example

A 1 m × 0.5 m × 0.2 m solid block has a volume of 0.1 m³. At an entered density of 500 kg/m³, its estimated mass is 50 kg and its weight force is about 490.33 N.

Assumptions and limitations

This models a solid cuboid with uniform density. It does not calculate a hollow box, sheet-metal enclosure, packaging, cavities, openings, joints or attached hardware. Those need additional geometry and material inputs. The calculator does not choose a density for you; use the density for the specific grade and condition of your material.

In everyday usage, mass is often reported in kilograms or pounds as “weight.” The newton and pound-force outputs are force values under standard gravity.

Frequently asked questions

How do I estimate the weight of a rectangular block?

Multiply its length, width and height to find volume, then multiply by the material density to estimate mass. This calculator also converts that mass to a force under standard gravity.

Can I use this for a hollow rectangular box?

No. This calculator assumes a solid cuboid. A hollow enclosure needs the volume of its material walls and any separate lid, base or hardware calculated from its construction dimensions.

Where should I get the density?

Use a reliable material specification for the exact material grade and condition, or measure a representative sample. Density can vary between materials sold under the same broad name.
