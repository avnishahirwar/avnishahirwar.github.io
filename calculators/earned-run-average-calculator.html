Earned Run Average (ERA) Calculator

Calculate ERA per nine innings using earned runs and recorded outs. Enter outs as a whole count to avoid treating baseball’s .1 and .2 inning notation as decimal tenths.

Formula and method

ERA = (earned runs × 9) ÷ innings pitched. For exact outs, innings = outs ÷ 3, so ERA = earned runs × 27 ÷ outs. Baseball notation .1 and .2 means one and two outs, not decimal tenths.

The calculation runs in your browser. Review the inputs, units and assumptions before relying on the result.

Worked example

For 3 earned runs and 17 outs (5⅔ innings): ERA = 3×27 ÷ 17 = 81 ÷ 17 ≈ 4.76 earned runs per nine innings.

Scope and limitations

  • Use earned runs officially charged to the pitcher under the applicable scoring rules; this calculator does not determine whether a run is earned.
  • ERA is a descriptive statistic, not a complete measure of pitching ability. Defense, park, league and role affect interpretation.
  • When no outs are recorded, ERA is undefined; the calculator reports that rather than dividing by zero.
  • ERA is normalized to nine innings and is not the same as runs per scheduled game.

Frequently asked questions

How do I enter 5⅔ innings?

Enter 17 outs. Three outs make one inning, so 17 outs equal five innings and two outs.

What happens with zero outs?

ERA has a zero denominator and is undefined until at least one out has been recorded.
