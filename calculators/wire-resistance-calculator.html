Wire Resistance Calculator

Calculate wire resistance and voltage drop from material resistivity, length, cross-sectional area and current.

Enter your values

Formula and methodology

Resistance R = ρ × L ÷ A, with area in square metres. Voltage drop = I × R.

Worked example

With Resistivity 0, Wire length 50, Cross-sectional area 2.5, Current 10, the calculator gives: Wire resistance (ohms) = 0.336; Voltage drop (V) = 3.36; Power lost as heat (W) = 33.6.

How to read the result

The default is copper at about 20 °C (1.68 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m). Resistance rises with temperature, and for a two-wire circuit you should use the total round-trip length.

Frequently asked questions

What is the resistivity of aluminium?

About 2.65 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m at 20 °C, higher than copper.

Should length be one way or both ways?

Use the full conductor length in the circuit, which is twice the cable run for a two-wire supply.

Assumptions and limitations

This tool uses idealised textbook formulas for learning and quick estimates. Real systems include friction, losses, tolerances and measurement error. Do not use it as the only basis for safety-critical or regulated engineering design.
