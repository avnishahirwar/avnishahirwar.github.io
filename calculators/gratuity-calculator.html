What does this gratuity calculator estimate?

It gives a simplified estimate using the monthly basic pay plus dearness allowance (DA) you enter and the service duration you enter. It is intended to help an employee understand the arithmetic behind a possible gratuity amount, not to determine legal eligibility or the amount an employer must pay.

Who may use it and what the inputs mean

Employees reviewing a payslip, planning a job transition or checking an employer's indicative calculation may use this estimate.

  • Monthly basic + DA: enter the eligible last-drawn monthly wage components used for the applicable gratuity calculation. Do not automatically use gross salary or total CTC.
  • Completed service years: enter the service duration in years for the estimate. The calculator applies the value entered directly; it does not determine continuous service, round partial years under the applicable legal rule, or verify employment records.

Formula and worked example

The standard monthly-rated illustration is: Gratuity = eligible monthly wages × 15 ÷ 26 × eligible years of service.

Example: with eligible monthly wages of ₹25,000 and five years entered, the estimate is ₹25,000 × 15 ÷ 26 × 5 = ₹72,115.38, before any applicable statutory ceiling or other legal adjustment. This example demonstrates the formula only; it is not an eligibility decision.

Common mistakes and real-life use

  • Entering total CTC or gross salary instead of the eligible wage basis.
  • Assuming every allowance is included in the wage base.
  • Entering a rounded service duration without checking the employer's service record and the applicable rule for a part-year.
  • Assuming the calculator checks statutory eligibility, maximum limits, tax treatment or special employment categories.

Example use: an employee can enter the eligible wage figure and service duration shown in their employment records to create a first-pass estimate, then compare it with the employer's written calculation.

Limitations and official references

The result does not establish coverage, continuous service, qualifying events, statutory wage definition, ceiling, tax treatment, fixed-term employment treatment, seasonal employment rules or exceptions. Eligibility and calculation can depend on the law in force, employment category and facts of the case. Seek clarification from the employer or an appropriate labour authority for a disputed amount.

Read the Ministry of Labour and Employment gratuity FAQs and the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972. Official text and applicable rules should be checked for the relevant employment situation.
