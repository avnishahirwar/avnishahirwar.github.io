Who should use this NPS calculator?

This estimator is for readers who want to understand how a regular monthly contribution and an assumed growth rate could build an illustrative retirement corpus. It can help compare contribution and time-period scenarios. It is not an NPS account statement, pension quote, or recommendation to invest.

What each input means

  • Monthly contribution: the recurring amount used in this projection. Actual contribution frequency and minimums depend on the account and applicable NPS rules.
  • Assumed annual return: a modelling assumption, not a promised or guaranteed NPS return. NPS investments are market-linked.
  • Investment period: the number of years the monthly contributions are projected to continue.
  • Annuity allocation: the percentage of the estimated corpus you choose to model as used to buy an annuity. This input is a scenario setting, not a statement of the legal exit percentage for every NPS subscriber.
  • Assumed annuity rate: a simplified annual rate used to illustrate a possible pension amount. Actual annuity quotations depend on provider, option, age and prevailing terms.

NPS formula and worked example

For monthly contribution P, monthly rate r = annual assumed return ÷ 12 ÷ 100, and n = months, the calculator estimates the future value as FV = P × [((1 + r)n − 1) ÷ r] × (1 + r). The final factor models contributions at the beginning of each month. When r is zero, the corpus is simply P × n.

Example: ₹5,000 per month for 25 years at an assumed 9% annual return produces an illustrative corpus of about ₹56.48 lakh in this model. If you select a 40% annuity allocation and a 6% assumed annuity rate, the calculator estimates the annuity portion and a simplified monthly amount. These are assumptions for demonstration, not a forecast or actual pension offer.

Common mistakes and real-life use

  • Do not treat an assumed return as a fixed or guaranteed rate.
  • Do not assume every NPS sector and exit event has the same lump-sum and annuity rules.
  • Do not compare a pre-tax projection with an after-tax amount from another product.
  • Check whether contributions, charges, asset allocation, inflation and the selected annuity option are represented before comparing scenarios.

A subscriber nearing retirement can use the calculator to test how changing the contribution or investment period changes the estimated corpus, then check the applicable exit rules and annuity quotations separately.

Limitations and official references

This simplified model does not reproduce NPS fund NAVs, asset-class returns, charges, tax treatment, partial withdrawals, contribution interruptions, sector-specific exit conditions or an insurer's annuity pricing. Actual NPS outcomes depend on scheme rules and market performance. Verify the current rules before acting.

Official references: PFRDA, NPS exit information, and the PFRDA exit regulations.

Frequently asked questions

Does this calculator predict the return on an NPS account?

No. It uses the annual return you enter as a fixed mathematical assumption. NPS returns are market-linked and can vary.

Does the annuity allocation field show the mandatory legal percentage?

No. It is a scenario input. Applicable exit and annuitisation rules depend on the subscriber category, exit reason, corpus and regulations in force. Check current PFRDA rules.

Is the estimated monthly annuity a provider quote?

No. It is a simplified calculation from the annuity allocation and rate you enter. Obtain an actual quotation from an authorised annuity service provider.
