Stock average formula

Average price = (Qty 1 × Price 1 + Qty 2 × Price 2) ÷ (Qty 1 + Qty 2)

Example: You bought 100 shares at ₹500 and then 50 more at ₹400. Total cost = ₹50,000 + ₹20,000 = ₹70,000. Total shares = 150. Average price = ₹70,000 ÷ 150 = ₹466.67. The share now needs to rise only to ₹466.67 for you to break even, instead of ₹500.

Frequently asked questions

What is averaging down in stocks?

Averaging down means buying more shares of a stock you already own after its price falls, which lowers your average buy price.

Do brokerage and taxes change my average price?

Yes. Brokerage, STT, GST and stamp duty add to your real cost. Add them to the purchase amount for a more accurate average.

Can I calculate the price needed to reach a target average?

Yes. Target price for new shares = (Target average × total shares after buying − current total cost) ÷ new shares to buy.

Does averaging guarantee a profit?

No. Averaging lowers your break-even price but increases your money in that stock. Review the company before buying more.
