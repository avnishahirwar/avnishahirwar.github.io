What this calculator does

Formula and calculation method

The calculator applies a periodic return derived from the annual assumption to recurring contributions. It assumes a steady rate only for illustration.

Guide: understanding your SIP Calculator results

How the SIP formula works

A SIP invests a fixed amount every month. This tool assumes a constant return compounded monthly and each instalment made at the end of the month. The future value is FV = P × [((1 + i)^n − 1) ÷ i], where i is the monthly return (annual ÷ 12) and n the number of months. At 12% a year, ₹5,000 a month for 10 years invests ₹6,00,000 and grows to about ₹11,50,193.

The effect of time

Doubling the period more than quadruples the outcome. The same ₹5,000 a month for 20 years invests ₹12,00,000 and reaches about ₹49,46,277, so returns of ₹37,46,277 come mainly from the second decade. Starting early matters more than choosing a slightly higher return.

What the estimate cannot tell you

Equity fund returns are not fixed. They can be negative for years. The 12% in the example is an assumption, not a promise, and real performance depends on the fund, the market and when you invest. Tax on gains, the expense ratio and exit load are not included. Use a range such as 8%, 10% and 12% to see a realistic band.

Worked example with real numbers

₹5,000 a month at an assumed 12% for 10 years: invested ₹6,00,000, estimated value ₹11,50,193.45 and estimated returns ₹5,50,193.45. For 20 years: invested ₹12,00,000, estimated value ₹49,46,276.83.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Treating the assumed return as guaranteed.
  • Stopping the SIP in a market fall, which removes the benefit of buying at lower prices.
  • Ignoring inflation when comparing the final amount with a goal.
  • Not increasing the SIP as income rises.

Important information

Returns are not guaranteed. Fees, taxes, market movements and contribution timing affect actual outcomes. This is not investment advice.

Frequently asked questions about the SIP Calculator

What return should I assume?

Use conservative assumptions for planning, for example 8–10% for equity-oriented funds over long periods, and test lower values too. Actual returns can be lower or higher.

Is the maturity value taxable?

Gains on mutual funds are taxed under capital gains rules, which depend on the fund type and holding period. Check the current rates before relying on the figure.

What is a step-up SIP?

A SIP whose amount rises every year, for example by 10%. It reaches a goal faster than a flat SIP, but this calculator models a flat amount.

SIP or lump sum?

A lump sum put in on day one grows for longer, but a SIP spreads the entry price over time and suits people who invest from monthly income.

How is this different from the FD calculator?

An FD compounds a single deposit at a fixed rate. A SIP is a series of monthly investments at a variable, assumed rate.
