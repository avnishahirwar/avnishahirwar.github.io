Frequently asked questions

What is a Systematic Investment Plan (SIP)?

A SIP is a way to invest a chosen amount at regular intervals, commonly monthly, in a mutual fund. It is a contribution method, not a separate product or a guarantee of returns.

How does this SIP calculator estimate future value?

It applies the assumed annual return as a monthly rate to the number of monthly contributions entered. This page assumes each contribution is made at the beginning of the month and the assumed rate stays constant.

Are SIP returns fixed or guaranteed?

No. Mutual-fund returns are market-linked and can rise or fall. The annual return entered here is only an assumption for a mathematical illustration, not a promised or expected return.

What happens if I enter a 0% annual return?

The estimated value equals total contributions because the calculator applies no growth. Total contributions are the monthly amount multiplied by the number of months.

Does this calculator assume contributions at the start or end of each month?

It assumes contributions are made at the beginning of each month. A different contribution date can produce a different estimate.

Are fund fees, taxes and inflation included?

No. The estimate does not model mutual-fund expenses, exit loads, taxes, inflation, missed instalments or changing returns. These factors can affect the actual value and purchasing power.

Can this calculator predict the future performance of a mutual fund?

No. It is a scenario calculator based on user-entered assumptions. It does not use live market data, assess a fund or predict future performance.

Does using a SIP remove investment risk?

No. Investing at regular intervals does not remove market risk or prevent losses. The risks depend on the mutual-fund scheme and its underlying investments.

Does this calculator provide investment advice?

No. It provides an educational estimate only. Review official scheme documents and consider qualified, appropriately registered advice for personal financial decisions.

How to read a SIP projection

If you invest ₹2,000 every month for 10 years, your own contributions total ₹2,40,000 before returns. A projected value above or below that amount depends on the assumed return and the calculator's contribution timing convention. Try several return assumptions to understand how sensitive the illustration is.

Important limits of the projection

Mutual-fund returns are market-linked and are not fixed or guaranteed. The projection does not predict future performance and may not include expense ratios, exit loads, taxes or missed instalments. Do not select a fund or make an investment solely from this estimate; review scheme documents and consider qualified financial advice.

SIP calculator guide: contributions and illustrative growth

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) is a method of investing a chosen amount at regular intervals, often monthly, into a mutual fund. This calculator projects how contributions could accumulate under a user-entered assumed return. It is a mathematical illustration, not a forecast, guarantee or recommendation.

How the SIP projection works

For a contribution made at the beginning of each month, a common model is FV = M × [((1 + i)n − 1) ÷ i] × (1 + i), where M is monthly contribution, i is assumed monthly return as a decimal and n is number of monthly contributions. A simplified conversion is annual assumed rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100. At a zero assumed return, future value equals total contributions.

Understand the result

Total contributions equal monthly amount multiplied by the number of months. Projected gain is estimated future value minus total contributions. For example, ₹2,000 per month for 10 years means 120 contributions and total contributions of ₹2,40,000. Any additional projected amount comes only from the assumed return in the model.

Why real outcomes vary

  • Market returns are uneven and can be negative over some periods.
  • Fund expenses, taxes, exit loads and tracking differences affect outcomes.
  • Contribution dates and purchase NAVs vary.
  • Equity, debt and hybrid schemes carry different risks.
  • Inflation changes the purchasing power of a future amount.

Use scenarios, not promises

Test conservative, moderate and higher assumptions rather than relying on one optimistic rate. Review the scheme's official documents, riskometer, benchmark, portfolio and costs. Past performance does not guarantee future results. The calculator does not model volatility, missed instalments, tax, fees or changing contributions. Consult an appropriately registered adviser for personalised advice.
