How CTC breaks down

Monthly take-home = (CTC − employer PF − gratuity provision) ÷ 12 − employee PF per month − professional tax − TDS
Example: ₹12 lakh CTC, 40% basic → basic ₹4.8L, employee PF ₹57,600, gratuity provision ₹23,088 → monthly gross cash ≈ ₹93,276; after employee PF, take-home is ≈ ₹88,476 before professional tax and TDS, assuming employer and employee PF are both 12% of basic.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between CTC and in-hand salary?

CTC is the total cost to company including employer contributions and benefits. In-hand is what reaches your account after deductions.

Is gratuity part of CTC?

Often yes, as an accrued provision (about 4.81% of basic). It is payable only after 5 years of continuous service.

Why is my actual in-hand different?

Employers structure salary differently — allowances, variable pay, insurance and perquisites all change the split. Use your salary structure sheet for exact numbers.

CTC to in-hand salary guide: understand your offer

Cost to Company (CTC) is the employer's annual cost associated with an employee. It is not necessarily the amount deposited into the employee's bank account. CTC may include fixed salary, variable pay, employer contributions, insurance premiums, gratuity provisions and other benefits. In-hand salary is the amount received after applicable deductions and payroll adjustments.

Common parts of a CTC package

  • Fixed cash components such as basic pay and allowances.
  • Variable pay or performance bonus, which may not be paid monthly.
  • Employer contributions to retirement or social-security benefits.
  • Gratuity or insurance costs included in employer cost.
  • Employee deductions such as PF, applicable professional tax and income-tax withholding.

Why CTC and bank credit differ

Some components are paid only later or when conditions are met. Employer contributions are not the same as employee take-home pay. Variable pay may be annual or performance dependent. Payroll deductions and tax withholding reduce monthly net pay. Dividing annual CTC by twelve is therefore not a reliable take-home estimate.

How to read an offer letter

Separate fixed cash compensation from variable pay and employer-paid benefits. Identify basic salary, allowances, employee and employer PF, gratuity treatment, insurance, bonus conditions and joining or retention benefits. Ask HR for a sample monthly payslip and a written breakup.

Compare offers consistently

Enter values exactly as described and avoid mixing annual and monthly figures. Compare fixed take-home, variable-pay conditions, benefits, retirement contributions, location costs and role expectations—not headline CTC alone. Tax depends on the applicable year, regime and personal circumstances; statutory deductions depend on eligibility and current rules.

Limitations

This calculator cannot validate payroll policy or guarantee a bank-credit amount. Request clarification in writing for unclear components and confirm the net-pay estimate with HR or a qualified tax professional.

Results are estimates based on entered values and assumptions. They are not professional advice. Verify important decisions with the relevant provider or a qualified professional.

Worked example: why CTC is not monthly take-home pay

Imagine an offer showing annual CTC of ₹12,00,000. That headline may include fixed cash salary, variable pay, employer retirement contributions, gratuity accrual and insurance or other benefits. These components do not all arrive in the employee's bank account every month. For example, if ₹1,00,000 of the annual package is conditional variable pay, ₹72,000 is employer PF, ₹28,800 is an indicative gratuity component and ₹12,000 is an employer-paid benefit, the fixed cash component is not the same as ₹12,00,000. Employee-side PF, applicable professional tax and income-tax withholding can reduce eventual bank credit further. This is an illustrative breakdown, not a standard salary structure.

Questions to ask HR before accepting an offer

  • Which parts of CTC are fixed, variable, conditional or paid only once a year?
  • Is basic salary the basis for PF and gratuity calculations, and what contribution rules apply?
  • Are employer PF, gratuity, insurance or reimbursements included in CTC?
  • What is the expected monthly gross cash salary, and what deductions appear on a sample payslip?
  • When is variable pay earned and paid, and can it depend on performance or joining conditions?
  • Which tax year and regime assumptions were used for any take-home estimate?

Tax and payroll assumptions

Income-tax withholding depends on the relevant tax year, employee declarations, eligible deductions and exemptions, other income and payroll processing. Statutory deductions can depend on location, establishment, salary components and eligibility. This calculator cannot inspect an employment contract, verify payroll setup or retrieve government records. For tax details, use the Income Tax Department portal; for provident-fund rules, refer to EPFO. Ask HR or payroll for a written component-wise explanation if the offer and payslip differ.

Common salary comparison mistakes

  • Comparing annual CTC from one employer with monthly gross salary from another.
  • Counting conditional variable pay as guaranteed monthly income.
  • Forgetting employee-side deductions and tax withholding.
  • Assuming employer contributions are part of monthly bank credit.
  • Comparing packages without considering role, location, benefits and payment timing.

Use the result as a planning estimate. The employer's written compensation structure, applicable law and actual payslip determine the final amount.
