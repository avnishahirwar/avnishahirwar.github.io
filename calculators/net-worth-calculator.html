Net worth formula

Net Worth = Total Assets − Total Liabilities
Assets are the current value of what you own. Liabilities are the outstanding amounts you owe.

Simple worked example

Suppose your cash and bank savings are ₹25,000, investments are ₹50,000, and other assets are ₹10,000. Your total assets are ₹85,000. If you have ₹20,000 in outstanding loan and card balances, your estimated net worth is ₹65,000 (₹85,000 − ₹20,000).

How to use this calculator

  1. Choose your currency. Indian rupees are selected automatically.
  2. Enter the current value of each asset, such as cash, savings, investments, property and vehicles.
  3. Enter the remaining balances on loans, credit cards and other liabilities.
  4. Select Calculate net worth to review total assets, total liabilities and the difference.
  5. Update the figures whenever your estimates change. Use consistent valuation dates.

Use current estimated values rather than the original purchase price. For a property or vehicle, use a reasonable current market estimate. For a loan, enter the amount still outstanding rather than the original amount borrowed.

What counts as an asset?

Assets are resources that have a current monetary value. Common examples include cash, bank account balances, fixed deposits, publicly held investments, retirement balances, property, land, vehicles and other valuable items. This simple calculator groups them into broad categories so you can enter a total without maintaining a detailed inventory.

What counts as a liability?

Liabilities are outstanding obligations. They may include a home loan, vehicle loan, personal loan, education loan, credit-card balance or other unpaid amount. Enter each balance once. Do not count a loan again if it is already included in another category.

How often should net worth be reviewed?

You can review it periodically, such as every few months, using values measured on the same date. A change in estimated net worth may reflect changes in asset prices, savings, borrowing or repayments. One calculation is only a snapshot and does not show cash flow, income stability or whether an asset can be sold quickly.

Net worth calculator FAQs

Can net worth be negative?

Yes. If total liabilities are greater than total assets, the calculated net worth will be negative. The result is a mathematical snapshot of the values entered.

Should I enter the original value or today's value?

Use a reasonable estimate of the current value of each asset, and use the remaining balance for each liability. Keep the valuation date consistent when comparing results over time.

Are investments guaranteed to keep their current value?

No. Market-linked investments can rise or fall. The calculator simply totals the values you enter and does not forecast returns or recommend products.

Does this tool store my entries?

No. The calculation runs locally in your browser. Do not enter account numbers, passwords or other confidential identifiers.
