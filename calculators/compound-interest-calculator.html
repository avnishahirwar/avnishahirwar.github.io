Compound interest formula

A = P × (1 + r/n)nt
Compound interest = A − P

Here, P is the initial principal, r is the annual interest rate expressed as a decimal, n is the number of compounding periods per year and t is the number of years. The calculator applies the selected frequency to the entire period.

Worked example: ₹1,00,000 at 8% for 5 years

With quarterly compounding, the rate per period is 8% ÷ 4 and there are 20 periods in five years. The formula compounds each period's interest into the balance. The final amount is therefore higher than simple interest under the same nominal rate and time assumptions.

What changes the estimate?

  • A higher assumed annual rate increases the illustrated final amount.
  • A longer period gives interest more time to compound.
  • Compounding frequency changes how often interest is added to the balance.
  • Fees, taxes, changing rates, deposits and withdrawals are not included.

Frequently asked questions

What is compound interest?

Compound interest is calculated on the original principal plus interest previously added to the balance.

Is this a bank or investment quote?

No. It is a mathematical illustration based on the fixed rate and compounding frequency you enter. Actual products can have different terms, tax treatment, fees and rate changes.

Does it include monthly contributions?

No. This tool models a one-time initial principal. Use a recurring-deposit or SIP calculator for periodic contribution scenarios, while reviewing their stated assumptions.

Does more frequent compounding always mean a higher result?

For a positive nominal rate held constant over the same period, more frequent compounding generally increases the mathematical result. Product terms may define rates and calculations differently.

Results are estimates, not financial advice or a promise of returns. Verify product terms, rates, fees and tax treatment with the relevant institution.
