Worked example

If current assets are ₹5,00,000 and current liabilities are ₹3,00,000, net working capital is ₹2,00,000. The current ratio is ₹5,00,000 ÷ ₹3,00,000 = approximately 1.67.

Frequently asked questions

What is net working capital?

It is current assets minus current liabilities. It is a snapshot of short-term operating resources after short-term obligations.

What does a current ratio above 1 mean?

It means current assets exceed current liabilities at the measurement date. It does not guarantee that assets can be converted to cash quickly.

Which items count as current assets?

Typical items include cash, receivables and inventory expected to be realized or used in the operating cycle. Follow the accounting basis used for your statements.

Can a very high current ratio be a concern?

Possibly. Excess inventory, slow receivables or idle cash can affect efficiency. Interpret the ratio with cash-flow and industry context.
