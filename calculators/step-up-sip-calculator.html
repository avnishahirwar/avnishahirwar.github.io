Step-up SIP: how the projection works, with an example

Formula

Each month, the contribution for year k is Monthly SIP × (1 + step-up)^(k − 1). The balance grows each month at the effective monthly rate (1 + annual return)^(1/12) − 1, and each contribution is added at the start of its month.

Worked example

Starting with ₹5,000 a month, increasing the SIP by 10% every year, at an assumed 12% annual return for 15 years: total contributions are about ₹19,06,349 and the estimated value is about ₹41,37,359. A flat ₹5,000 SIP with no step-up under the same assumptions invests ₹9,00,000 and reaches about ₹23,79,657. Returns are illustrative, not guaranteed.

Inputs explained

  • Starting monthly SIP: the amount in the first year.
  • Annual increase (%): how much you raise the SIP every 12 months, for example in line with salary growth.
  • Assumed annual return (%): an assumption, not a promise; market-linked returns vary and can be negative in some periods.
  • Period (years): longer periods let compounding do more of the work.

Common mistakes

  • Using an optimistic return assumption and treating it as a forecast.
  • Choosing a step-up you cannot sustain if income does not rise as expected.
  • Ignoring expense ratio, exit load and taxes, which are not modelled here.
  • Comparing a step-up result with a flat SIP of a different period.

Frequently asked questions

What is a step-up SIP?

A SIP whose monthly amount increases by a fixed percentage at regular intervals, usually every year.

Why does a step-up help?

Later contributions are larger, so more money is invested overall and the final corpus is usually higher than with a flat SIP.

Are the results guaranteed?

No. Returns on market-linked investments are not guaranteed and this tool uses a constant assumed rate.

Does it include taxes and fees?

No. Fund expenses, exit loads and capital-gains tax are excluded.
