Guide to the SWP Calculator: method, example and FAQs

What a systematic withdrawal plan does

A systematic withdrawal plan takes a fixed amount out of an investment at regular intervals, usually every month, while the rest stays invested and keeps earning. It is the mirror image of a SIP and is commonly used to create a steady income from a corpus, for example in retirement.

With monthly rate i = annual rate ÷ 12, the balance after n withdrawals of W is Balance = P(1 + i)n − W × [(1 + i)n − 1] ÷ i. The number of months the money lasts is n = −ln(1 − P·i ÷ W) ÷ ln(1 + i), which only exists when W is larger than the monthly return P·i.

Worked example

Start with ₹10,00,000 earning an assumed 8% a year, and withdraw ₹10,000 every month. The monthly return is ₹6,667, so the withdrawal is larger than the growth and the corpus slowly shrinks. After 5 years the balance is ₹7,55,077, and the money lasts about 165 months, or 13.8 years.

If the withdrawal is cut to ₹6,667 or less, the corpus never falls: it only pays out the return. That figure, the sustainable withdrawal, is the key number for anyone planning retirement income.

Choosing a withdrawal rate

A rate that looks small, 4% to 6% of the starting corpus a year, tends to last longer; rates near or above the expected return drain the corpus. Costs, tax on the gains portion of each withdrawal and rising expenses all shorten the life of the money, so test the calculator at a lower return than you hope for.

Things people overlook

  • Each withdrawal sells units, and the gain portion is taxable according to the fund type and holding period.
  • Expenses rise with inflation, so a fixed withdrawal loses buying power; some plans step the amount up each year.
  • Poor returns in the first few years hurt far more than the same returns later, because withdrawals shrink the base that has to recover.

Frequently asked questions

What does SWP stand for?

Systematic Withdrawal Plan: a fixed sum withdrawn from an investment at set intervals.

How long will my corpus last?

It depends on the return and the withdrawal. For ₹10,00,000 at 8% with ₹10,000 a month, about 13.8 years.

Is SWP income guaranteed?

No. The return is an assumption; if actual returns are lower the corpus will run down sooner.

SWP or fixed deposit interest payout?

A deposit pays a declared interest and leaves the principal intact; an SWP can pay more but depends on market returns and may reduce the principal.
