How the EMI is estimated

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1), where P is principal, r is the monthly interest rate and n is the number of monthly payments.

For this example, P = ₹5,00,000, r = 10% ÷ 12 ÷ 100, and n depends on the selected tenure. For a 2-year term, n = 24 and the estimated EMI is about ₹23,072.

What this estimate excludes

  • Processing fees, insurance, taxes and other lender charges
  • Floating-rate changes, missed payments or prepayments
  • Different lender conventions for payment dates and rounding

Actual EMI and total cost depend on the lender’s amortisation schedule and loan agreement.

Frequently asked questions

What is the EMI on a ₹5,00,000 loan at 10% for 2 years?

About ₹23,072 per month under the assumptions above, with roughly ₹53,739 paid as interest over the term.

Does a longer tenure reduce the EMI?

Usually yes, because the principal is spread over more monthly payments. However, the total interest paid is generally higher.

Is the interest rate fixed for the full loan?

This illustration assumes a fixed rate. A floating-rate loan can change when the lender revises its benchmark or spread.

Will my bank quote exactly this EMI?

Not necessarily. Fees, payment dates, rate type, rounding and lender-specific terms can change the final schedule.
